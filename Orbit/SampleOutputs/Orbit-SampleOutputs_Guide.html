Orbit

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.7.0  |  Build a1a3dbb90d3b  |  Updated 24 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide is for an instructor who has the Orbit Help open. It shows one figure for each Beat of the Help, made with a command from that Beat, together with the values the run prints. It is not a retelling of the tutorial. No external image files are required.

Orbit follows a test particle in the Newtonian inverse-square field of a fixed central mass with an adaptive predictor-corrector integrator. Every run prints a summary of five-significant-digit diagnostics and then opens the plot selected by --output.

Beat 0 — A planet in a fixed field

python main.py

The default run starts a Mercury-like particle at periapsis, 4.6×1010 m from the central mass, moving at 58 980 m/s in the +y direction, and follows it for one revolution.

What it shows

  • The path is one closed oval, not a circle, with the central mass at a focus nearer the starting end.
  • The Keplerian block describes the starting state exactly; the run then reproduces that orbit to a small numerical error.
  • The run stops when the accumulated azimuth reaches one full turn, with the last step refined to land on it.

Headline values

  • Accepted steps 761; elapsed simulated time 7.6025×106 s, against a Keplerian period of 7.6019×106 s.
  • Periapsis radius 4.6000×1010 m; apoapsis radius 6.9832×1010 m; eccentricity 0.20575.
  • Specific energy -1.1457×109 J/kg at the start and -1.1457×109 J/kg at the end.
  • Max fractional energy drift 5.3444×10−5; closure residuals 5.2282×10−9 (radius) and 4.2156×10−5 (velocity).

Beat 1 — Energy decides bound or unbound

python main.py --vyInit 85000 --maxSteps 600

At 85 000 m/s, above the local escape speed of 75 961 m/s, the specific energy is positive and the path is open.

What it shows

  • The particle passes the central mass once and keeps receding; the run ends at the 600-step limit, not at a revolution.
  • The sign of the starting specific energy sorts the three speeds of the Help: negative for 70 000 m/s, nearly zero for 75 961 m/s, positive here.
  • Apoapsis radius and Keplerian period print as n/a (unbound); two extra lines give the final radius and speed.

Headline values

  • This run: hyperbolic, specific energy 7.2745×108 J/kg, eccentricity 1.5043; final radius 3.2231×1011 m, final speed 47735 m/s.
  • --vyInit 70000: elliptic, specific energy -4.3505×108 J/kg, eccentricity 0.69841, apoapsis radius 2.5905×1011 m.
  • --vyInit 75961 --maxSteps 600: specific energy -16283 J/kg, but max absolute specific-energy drift 98844 J/kg.
  • In that near-escape run the final specific energy is 82138 J/kg: the computed energy has changed sign, so the run cannot show whether the orbit returns.

Beat 2 — Speed sets size, direction sets shape

python main.py --vxInit 41705.15 --vyInit 41705.15

The same speed as the default run, aimed 45° from the y-axis, gives an orbit of the same size but a very different shape.

What it shows

  • The energy is unchanged, so the semimajor axis and period are the same as in Beat 0; the angular momentum is smaller, so the orbit is far more elongated.
  • Periapsis is no longer on the x-axis; the particle starts part of the way round the ellipse.
  • The harder orbit costs accuracy: the drift and closure residuals are much larger than for the default run.

Headline values

  • Semimajor axis 5.7916×1010 m and Keplerian period 7.6019×106 s, identical to Beat 0.
  • Eccentricity 0.72192; periapsis radius 1.6105×1010 m; apoapsis radius 9.9726×1010 m.
  • Periapsis longitude 236.63 deg; initial true anomaly 123.37 deg.
  • Max fractional energy drift 0.0019332; closure radius residual 0.0013742.

Beat 3 — Equal areas and the period

python main.py --xInit 3.626e8 --vyInit 1082 --k 3.986e14 --dt0 1000

A Moon-like particle around a fixed Earth (--k 3.986e14) has a much shorter period than the Mercury-like one, yet obeys the same period–size relation.

What it shows

  • The Keplerian period and the integrated elapsed time agree, as they do for the Sun-centred orbit.
  • Multiplying T²/a³ by the central body’s GM gives 4π² for both bodies, which is Kepler’s third law.
  • Starting the default orbit at its far end with the right speed reproduces the same ellipse from the other end.

Headline values

  • Keplerian period 2.4034×106 s (27.8 days) and elapsed simulated time 2.4034×106 s.
  • Semimajor axis 3.8780×108 m; eccentricity 0.064989.
  • Max fractional energy drift 1.3642×10−6; closure radius residual 1.4503×10−11.
  • --xInit 6.9832e10 --vyInit 38851.5: eccentricity 0.20575, period 7.6019×106 s, periapsis longitude 180.00 deg.

Beat 4 — Position and velocity both repeat

python main.py --output velocity

The velocity vector of a Kepler orbit moves on a circle. The cross marks zero velocity and the red dot the initial velocity.

What it shows

  • The circle encloses the origin, which is the velocity-space signature of a bound orbit.
  • The start is the top of the circle, at the periapsis speed; the lowest point is the apoapsis speed.
  • Running the orbit clockwise (--vyInit -58980) puts the centre of the circle below the origin instead of above it.

Headline values

  • Closure residuals after one revolution: 5.2282×10−9 (radius) and 4.2156×10−5 (velocity); endpoint refinement trials 7.
  • --maxOrbits 2: 2.0868×10−8 and 8.4305×10−5; --maxOrbits 5: 1.3020×10−7 and 0.00021071.
  • The velocity residual grows in proportion to the number of revolutions, the radius residual as its square.
  • Clockwise run: the same closure residuals, 5.2282×10−9 and 4.2156×10−5.

Beat 5 — The corrector

python main.py --dt0 1e6 --eps1 10 --eps2 1e-12

With very long steps the trajectory is visibly a polygon; a strict eps2 makes the corrector work harder on each of them.

What it shows

  • The polygon has more, shorter sides than the 14-step run of Beat 6, because some trial steps failed to converge in ten corrector passes and were halved.
  • For the ordinary steps of the Help’s other commands eps2 does not change the accepted steps; it changes the number of acceleration evaluations.
  • The energy drift is set by the step length, not by eps2.

Headline values

  • This run: accepted steps 24, against 14 for the same command with the default eps2.
  • Acceleration evaluations for --eps2 10, 1e-4 (default) and 1e-8: 1534, 2301 and 3062, all with 761 accepted steps.
  • Closure radius residual for those three runs: 4.7641×10−7, 5.2282×10−9 and 5.2073×10−9.
  • This run: max fractional energy drift 0.071818; closure velocity residual 0.052612.

Beat 6 — The step control

python main.py --dt0 1e6 --eps1 10

With eps1 made powerless and steps up to 106 s, the particle takes 14 long steps and finishes with a velocity that is 25% wrong.

What it shows

  • With the default eps1 the controller shortens steps near periapsis and holds the orbit close to an oval; here it is not allowed to.
  • The angular-step safeguard, which eps1 does not switch off, rejects one trial step.
  • The printed step extremes show how far the controller shortened the steps in the other runs of the Help.

Headline values

  • Accepted steps 14; angular-step rejections 1; longest accepted step 1.0000×106 s.
  • Max fractional energy drift 0.21231; closure velocity residual 0.24791, a 25% error in the final velocity.
  • --dt0 1e5 (default eps1): 181 steps, longest accepted step 88595 s.
  • --dt0 1e5 --eps1 10: 77 steps, max fractional energy drift 0.0053649.

Beat 7 — How far to trust it

python main.py --dt0 1250 --output energy

A drift of a few parts in 107 is invisible on the energy plot; the printed diagnostics are what show it.

What it shows

  • Kinetic and potential energy swap over the orbit while their total looks perfectly flat.
  • Halving dt0 divides the energy and velocity-closure drift by four and the radius closure by sixteen.
  • The way to judge a result is to repeat the run with a smaller dt0 and see whether the numbers stop changing.

Headline values

  • --dt0 1250: accepted steps 6082; max fractional energy drift 8.3504×10−7; closure radius residual 1.2675×10−12.
  • --dt0 5000: 1521 steps, drift 1.3361×10−5.
  • --dt0 2500: 3041 steps, drift 3.3401×10−6.
  • Default --dt0 10000: 761 steps, drift 5.3444×10−5.

Beat 8 — The hard cases

python main.py --vyInit 10000

A start at 10 km/s instead of 58.98 km/s gives a very eccentric orbit that passes close to the central mass.

What it shows

  • Near periapsis the acceleration changes fast, so the step controller shortens the steps and the accuracy suffers.
  • Shortening dt0 does not help there; tightening eps1 does.
  • A start with no sideways velocity runs straight into the point-mass singularity and stops at a numerical guard.

Headline values

  • This run: eccentricity 0.96534; periapsis radius 8.1127×108 m; accepted steps 579; max fractional energy drift 0.011176.
  • --dt0 1000: 2176 steps, drift 0.011729, no better.
  • --eps1 0.005: 5044 steps, drift 0.00010947.
  • Radial infall (--vxInit -1000 --vyInit 0): point-mass singularity approached after 1171 steps; max fractional energy drift 1.8426×105.

Notes for evaluation

  • Five views: the program supports orbit, velocity, position_time, velocity_time and energy. The two component-versus-time views are not shown here.
  • Figures and Beats: every command is a command from that Beat of the Help; where the Beat has several, the others are covered in the headline values. Beats 0 and 4 share the default run and use different displays; Beats 5 and 6 share a base command and use different settings.
  • Interface: all ten driver inputs and the display selector are command-line options. Run python main.py --help for their descriptions and defaults.
  • Model scope: the central body is fixed and point-like. The parameter k is its gravitational parameter GM in m³/s², not its mass in kilograms.
  • Reading the numbers: the drift and closure values describe the integration, and the Keplerian block describes the starting state exactly. A run that ends normally can still be untrustworthy, as Beats 6 and 8 show.
  • Verification: the automated suite of 156 tests passes for this build; it also runs every command shown in the Help.
