MercPert

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.1.0  |  Build 9852baf113f7  |  Updated 23 September 2026

Purpose. One figure for each Beat of the MercPert Beats Help, in Beat order, for an instructor reading alongside it. Each section gives the exact command, the plot it produces, and the values the program prints. Beat 9 has no figure: its four commands each stop with a one-line error or usage message before any plot is drawn. No external image files are required.

In every orbit plot, the dashed red curve is the Sun's prescribed circular path and the dashed green curve is the companion's; the integrated test-particle (“Mercury”) path is solid blue, in barycentric coordinates. Filled circles mark starting positions, and a black × marks a collision. Figure 5 is the one exception: it plots the fractional Jacobi-constant drift against time rather than an orbit.

Figure 1 — Beat 0 · A test particle in a binary

python main.py

The default 0.1-solar-mass companion turns what would be a closed Kepler ellipse into a precessing rosette that never quite retraces itself.

What it shows

  • The small dashed red circle is the Sun's own orbit about the centre of mass; the large dashed green circle is the companion's.
  • The solid blue loops swing between the printed Sun-distance range and slowly turn instead of closing.
  • The run covers 231.48 days, 1.13 times the 203.96-day binary period, which is why the pattern has visibly rotated by the end.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 10000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Circular period of Sun–companion pair: 203.96 days
  • Test particle integration time: 231.48 days (2.0000e+07 s)
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.30000 to 0.40762 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 1.2305e-06

Figure 2 — Beat 1 · The binary is prescribed

python main.py --m_planet_solar 0.5 --vy_init 54400

Making the companion half as massive as the Sun makes both prescribed circles large enough to see, and Mercury's path now swings between 0.14420 and 0.31804 AU from a Sun that is itself circling the centre of mass.

What it shows

  • Both dashed circles are now a substantial fraction of the plot, unlike Beat 0's nearly invisible Sun circle.
  • The blue loops swing across the whole printed Sun-distance range, since Mercury's 54,400 m/s launch speed is measured from a moving Sun.
  • The printed circular period, 174.66 days, is shorter than Beat 0's, matching Kepler's third law for the larger total mass.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 10000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Circular period of Sun–companion pair: 174.66 days
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.14420 to 0.31804 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 2.6890e-05
  • Eq. 2 predicts the Sun's own circle at 0.23333 AU and the companion's at 0.46667 AU from the centre of mass, both readable on the plot.

Figure 3 — Beat 2 · Switch the companion off

python main.py --m_planet_solar 1e-12

With the companion mass reduced to a negligible 10⁻¹² solar masses, Mercury traces a plain, closed Kepler ellipse around a Sun that no longer visibly moves.

What it shows

  • The Sun's own orbital radius is unmeasurably small at this companion mass, so no red dashed circle is visible, only the red dot.
  • The green dashed circle still marks the companion's prescribed path, now essentially the full 0.7 AU separation from the barycentre.
  • The blue ellipse closes on itself and does not precess, unlike Beat 0's rosette with the same launch speed.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 10000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.30000 to 0.43707 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 1.6385e-06
  • Eq. 4 predicts this aphelion from the 8.90%-supercircular launch speed: 0.43707 AU, matching to five figures.
  • The beat's other command launches at exactly the circular speed, 54,379.16 m/s, and prints a 0.30000 to 0.30000 AU range instead.

Figure 4 — Beat 3 · Turn the companion back up

python main.py --m_planet_solar 0.1 --vy_init 54379.16

At the default 0.1-solar-mass companion, the circular-speed launch of Beat 2 becomes a slightly off-centre ellipse ranging from 0.26271 to 0.31291 AU.

What it shows

  • The orbit is still a recognisable, slowly turning ellipse, not yet the tangle Beat 5 produces at longer run times.
  • The Sun-distance spread, 0.05020 AU, is about ten times the spread from a companion one-tenth as massive.
  • Doubling the companion mass by a further decade would give a spread near 0.5 AU, no longer a small perturbation.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 10000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.26271 to 0.31291 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 1.2668e-06
  • At 0.001 solar masses the range is 0.29965 to 0.30014 AU (spread 0.00049 AU).
  • At 0.01 solar masses the range is 0.29649 to 0.30140 AU (spread 0.00491 AU), about ten times the 0.001 case.

Figure 5 — Beat 4 · The one number that is conserved

python main.py --show_jacobi_diagnostic

Over the same default run, the Jacobi constant's fractional drift wanders within about a millionth of its initial value with no steady trend, while Mercury's ordinary energy changes by 31% (Beat 4's second command).

What it shows

  • The vertical scale is 1e-6; the curve oscillates between about -1.23e-6 and +6.6e-8 and keeps returning toward zero.
  • This is the same default run as Figure 1, plotted as drift in the conserved Jacobi constant rather than as position.
  • Beat 4's second command, a short Python one-liner, computes Mercury's ordinary barycentric energy for the identical run and finds it swings by 0.3110, 250,000 times more than the Jacobi drift.

Headline values

  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 1.2305e-06
  • One-liner energy swing over the same run: 0.3110 (31%)
  • Circular period of Sun–companion pair: 203.96 days
  • Test particle integration time: 231.48 days (2.0000e+07 s)

Figure 6 — Beat 5 · Close encounters and the step controller

python main.py --max_steps 40000

Run four times longer, the default system's orbit is pumped by the companion into a Sun-grazing tangle that comes within 0.016704 AU (3.6 solar radii) of the Sun.

What it shows

  • The neat, widely spaced loops of Figure 1 become a dense tangle crowding close to the Sun.
  • The closest approach, 0.016704 AU, is 3.6 solar radii from the Sun's centre.
  • The adaptive controller's working timestep falls to 199.63 s near that approach, a tenth of the 2000 s maximum it uses everywhere else.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 40000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.016704 to 0.51065 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 0.0051623
  • Adaptive controller working timestep range: 199.63 to 2000.0 s
  • The beat's second command tightens eps1 fivefold: its smallest step is 39.907 s and its Jacobi drift falls 23-fold, to 0.00022738.

Figure 7 — Beat 6 · Collision and escape

python main.py --m_planet_solar 1

Doubling the companion's mass to match the Sun sends Mercury into a collision with the Sun after 41.543 days, marked with a black X.

What it shows

  • The run stops early, after 1920 of up to 10,000 steps, with termination: collision with Sun.
  • The final Sun distance, 0.0046505 AU (6.9570e+08 m), is exactly the nominal solar radius used as the stopping surface.
  • The Sun's dashed reference circle is only partly drawn because the run lasted less than one 151.26-day binary period.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 1920; termination: collision with Sun
  • Test particle integration time: 41.543 days (3.5893e+06 s)
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.0046505 to 0.40201 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 0.0088234
  • The beat's other command starts Mercury at 0.45 AU instead of 0.30 AU: after 694.44 days it reaches 11.531 AU, more than sixteen times the binary separation.

Figure 8 — Beat 7 · Outside the binary

python main.py --m_planet_solar 1 --binary_separation_au 1 --x_init_au 2.5 --vy_init 50846 --max_steps 40000

With the correct circumbinary launch speed, 50,846 m/s measured from the Sun, Mercury goes around the outside of the equal-mass binary instead of falling into it.

What it shows

  • The blue path stays well clear of both dashed circles, circling the whole binary rather than either star alone.
  • The Sun's and companion's dashed circles are the same size here, each 0.5 AU from the centre of mass, since the two masses are equal.
  • The Sun-distance range, 1.5463 to 2.6554 AU, is close to but not exactly the 1.5 to 2.5 AU an exact 2-solar-mass point at the centre would give.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 40000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 1.5463 to 2.6554 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 1.4107e-09
  • the smallest drift on this page, because this orbit never comes near either star.
  • The beat's first command, 30,000 m/s Sun-relative, is only 8,939 m/s in the frame of the centre of mass: Mercury falls into the binary, reaching 0.27651 AU from the Sun.

Figure 9 — Beat 8 · How far to trust a run

python main.py --max_steps 80000 --dt 1000

Halving the maximum step for Beat 5's Sun-grazing run barely changes its Jacobi drift, 0.0051786 against 0.0051623, because near the Sun the adaptive controller, not --dt, already sets the step.

What it shows

  • The closest approach, 0.017065 AU, agrees with Beat 5's 0.016704 AU to about half a percent, so that result is robust.
  • The Jacobi drift barely improves despite halving --dt, because eps1, not --dt, is already setting the step size near the Sun.
  • On the smooth Beat 0 orbit, by contrast, halving --dt from 2000 to 1000 to 500 s divides the drift by about 4 each time.

Headline values

  • accepted steps: 80000; termination: max_steps reached
  • Test particle Sun-distance range: 0.017065 to 0.51112 AU
  • Maximum fractional Jacobi drift: 0.0051786
  • Adaptive controller working timestep range: 206.65 to 1000.0 s
  • The default orbit's three convergence runs (dt = 2000, 1000, 500 s) give drifts 1.2305e-06, 3.0312e-07 and 7.5216e-08: ratios 4.06 and 4.03, consistent with second-order convergence.

Notes for evaluation

  • Model scope: a planar circular restricted three-body model. The Sun and companion follow a prescribed circular orbit about their barycentre (Eqs. 2–3); the test particle has no back-reaction on either.
  • Input convention: the initial position and velocity are given relative to the Sun and converted to barycentric coordinates before integration (Eq. 10, Figure 7).
  • Numerical method: an Euler predictor is followed by an iterated trapezoidal corrector (Eqs. 7–8). The working timestep shrinks when the predicted acceleration changes too much or the corrector fails to converge, and grows back by 10% per accepted step.
  • Conserved quantity: the Jacobi constant (Eq. 6), not Mercury's ordinary energy, is what the exact equations keep fixed; its drift is a check on numerical error, not a bound on every error (Figure 4, Figure 5).
  • Collision handling: a configurable moving circular boundary can stop the trajectory at the Sun or the companion (Eq. 9). The default enables the nominal solar radius and disables a companion radius.
  • Termination: the driver stops at max_steps unless a configured collision occurs. Whether an orbit is escaping is an interpretation from the trajectory over a stated finite interval, not a separate automatic stop condition.
  • Build verification: version 1.1.0, build 9852baf113f7. The integration and display equations are unchanged since the previous release; commands and printed values are current.
