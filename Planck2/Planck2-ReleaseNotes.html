Known Limitations

  • Ideal black-body radiation at one temperature; no absorption lines, non-unit emissivity or radiative transfer.
  • Numerical integrals are trapezoidal sums over a finite dimensionless x-range. Default physical integrals differ from their exact zero-to-infinity references by less than 10−6 in relative magnitude.
  • The reported peak is a sampled maximum; the one-grid-step bound requires a range containing the true peak and switch settings that do not create a higher false maximum.
  • Moving approximation switches into inappropriate regions can deliberately produce false maxima and inaccurate integrals.
  • The radiance integral is in W m−2 sr−1; emitted surface flux is π times that integral. The energy-density integral is in J m−3.
  • Help equations require online MathJax; the trapezoidal illustration is an external link. Guide figures are embedded for offline viewing.

Known Minor Maintenance Items

  • The current test suite retains content-specific assertions for archived Help sections and wording; their failures and errors are listed in the validation report.
  • MathJax is not bundled for offline use.
  • The plot annotation rounds peak x to four decimal places; the console rounds it to six.
  • The --x_max help text does not explicitly state that --x_high must not exceed it.
  • No command-line figure-save option is provided.
