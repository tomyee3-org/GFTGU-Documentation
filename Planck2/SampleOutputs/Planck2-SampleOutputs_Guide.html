Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.5.1  |  Build 1107bea3441f  |  Updated 24 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide is for an instructor who has the Planck2 Help open. It shows one figure for each Beat of the Help, made with a command from that Beat, together with the values the run prints. It is not a retelling of the tutorial. No external image files are required.

Planck2 evaluates black-body spectra on a uniform grid in the dimensionless variable x = hc/(λkT) = hν/(kT) and maps them to physical wavelength or frequency. Every run prints a five-line summary (peak x, peak wavelength or frequency, dimensionless area, physical integral and its exact zero-to-infinity value) and then opens the plot, whose annotation box adds the peak value.

Beat 0 — One curve for a hot body

python main.py

The default run: spectral radiance per unit wavelength of a 5900 K black body, the approximate temperature of the Sun's visible surface.

What it shows

  • One hump, steep on the short-wavelength side and slow on the long side; the dotted line marks the sampled peak.
  • The displayed range is only where the curve exceeds 0.3% of its peak; the vertical axis starts at zero.
  • The dimensionless area and the physical integral are printed beside their exact zero-to-infinity values.

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 4.959505; peak wavelength 4.917033e-07 m; peak value 2.9281e+13 W m−3 sr−1 (annotation).
  • Dimensionless area 122.081167, exact 122.081167.
  • Physical integral 2.187105e+07 W m−2 sr−1, exact 2.187106e+07.

Beat 1 — Temperature slides the curve

python main.py --T 3000

The same law at 3000 K: the hump moves to longer wavelength and becomes far lower.

What it shows

  • The printed peak x is the same as at 5900 K; only the physical wavelength and the height change.
  • The peak moves into the near infrared, about twice the default wavelength.
  • Peak wavelength times temperature is the same at every temperature (Wien's displacement law).

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 4.959505; peak wavelength 9.670165e-07 m; peak value 9.9525e+11 W m−3 sr−1.
  • --T 10000: peak wavelength 2.901049e-07 m; peak value 4.0957e+14; peak x again 4.959505.
  • Peak value ratio 5900 K / 3000 K = 29.42, which is (5900/3000)5.
  • Physical integral 1.461997e+06 W m−2 sr−1 at 3000 K and 1.804935e+08 at 10000 K.

Beat 2 — Per wavelength or per frequency

python main.py --T 2.725 --quantity frequency

Spectral radiance per unit frequency at 2.725 K, the temperature of the cosmic microwave background.

What it shows

  • The frequency curve uses x3/(ex−1), so its peak is at a different x from the wavelength curve.
  • The peak falls in the microwave band, near 160 GHz.
  • For the same 5900 K body, c divided by the peak frequency is not the peak wavelength of Beat 0.

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 2.809720; peak frequency 1.595353e+11 Hz; peak value 3.8366e-18 W m−2 sr−1 Hz−1.
  • Physical integral 9.952395e-07 W m−2 sr−1, exact 9.952402e-07.
  • --T 5900 --quantity frequency: peak x = 2.809720, peak frequency 3.454159e+14 Hz.
  • c/νpeak at 5900 K = 867.9 nm, against the wavelength-curve peak of 491.7 nm.

Beat 3 — Radiance and energy density

python main.py --T 5900 --quantity energy_density

Spectral energy density per unit frequency at 5900 K: the same curve as the frequency radiance, scaled by 4π/c.

What it shows

  • The curve has the same shape and the same peak frequency as the 5900 K frequency radiance.
  • Only the vertical unit changes, to J m−3 Hz−1, and the physical integral is stored energy per volume in J m−3.
  • Peak values and integrals of the two quantities differ by the factor 4π/c = 4.1917×10−8 s m−1 sr.

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 2.809720; peak frequency 3.454159e+14 Hz; peak value 1.6323e-15 J m−3 Hz−1.
  • Physical integral 9.167666e-01 J m−3, exact 9.167672e-01.
  • Frequency radiance at 5900 K: peak value 3.8940e-08, physical integral 2.187105e+07 W m−2 sr−1.
  • --T 2.725 --quantity energy_density: physical integral 4.171735e-14 J m−3.

Beat 4 — Adding up the spectrum

python main.py --quantity frequency

The default body described per unit frequency. Its integrated radiance equals that of the wavelength curve of Beat 0.

What it shows

  • The two radiance curves look different but have the same physical integral, radiance summed over the spectrum.
  • The dimensionless areas differ (x3 against x5) and are not physical quantities.
  • The emitted flux per square metre is π times the radiance integral, which is σT4.

Headline values

  • Dimensionless area 6.493935, exact π4/15 = 6.493939.
  • Physical integral 2.187105e+07 W m−2 sr−1, the same as the wavelength run's 2.187105e+07; exact 2.187106e+07.
  • Wavelength run: dimensionless area 122.081167, exact 8π6/63 = 122.081167.
  • Relative error of the physical integral −6.9×10−7 (frequency) and −6.9×10−7 (wavelength).

Beat 5 — Doubling T multiplies the integral by 16

python main.py --T 12000 --quantity frequency

Frequency radiance at 12000 K, the hottest of the three doubling steps in this Beat.

What it shows

  • The peak frequency is proportional to T; the peak x is unchanged.
  • Each doubling of T multiplies the physical integral by 16, for radiance and energy density alike.
  • The ratio checks the T4 scaling, not the accuracy of the sum; the exact reference line checks accuracy.

Headline values

  • Peak frequency 7.025408e+14 Hz; physical integral 3.742713e+08 W m−2 sr−1.
  • Frequency radiance integrals at 3000, 6000, 12000 K: 1.461997e+06, 2.339196e+07, 3.742713e+08 (ratios 16.00 and 16.00).
  • Energy density integrals at 3000, 6000, 12000 K: 6.128240e-02, 9.805184e-01, 1.568829e+01 J m−3.
  • Integral / T4 at 3000 K: 1.8049×10−8 W m−2 sr−1 K−4, against σ/π = 1.8049×10−8.

Beat 6 — The sampled grid

python main.py --n_steps 10

With only ten steps of x the displayed curve is one straight segment, and the sampled peak lies at a shorter wavelength than the true peak.

What it shows

  • Eleven grid points cannot follow the hump; the printed peak is a grid point, not the true maximum.
  • The integrals fall far below their exact references.
  • Refining the grid brings the sums to the exact values and the peak to within one grid step of x = 4.965114.

Headline values

  • --n_steps 10: peak x = 10.009000; physical integral 1.521580e+06 against exact 2.187106e+07.
  • --n_steps 100: peak x = 5.009500; physical integral 2.185156e+07.
  • --n_steps 1000: peak x = 5.009500; physical integral 2.187101e+07.
  • --n_steps 20000: peak x = 4.964504; physical integral 2.187107e+07, slightly above the exact value.

Beat 7 — The approximation switches

python main.py --x_low 4

Using the Rayleigh–Jeans form up to x = 4 creates a false maximum at the switch, although the true peak lies in the exact branch.

What it shows

  • The true hump, near 0.5 µm, is still there but small; the printed peak is a false maximum just on the Rayleigh–Jeans side of the switch, at the jump near 0.62 µm.
  • The physical integral is several times its exact reference, which is the check that exposes the run.
  • A switch placed away from the true peak can still move the printed peak.

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 3.959605 (true peak 4.965114); peak value 3.3949e+14, against 2.9281e+13 by default.
  • Physical integral 7.989500e+07 W m−2 sr−1, 3.65 times the exact 2.187106e+07.
  • --x_low 5 --x_high 6: area 689.653453, physical integral 1.444356e+08, yet peak x = 4.959505.
  • --x_low 1 --x_high 3: area 121.281452, physical integral 2.201527e+07.

Beat 8 — The hard cases

python main.py --x_max 4 --x_high 3

Cutting the x-range at 4, below the true peak, leaves only the long-wavelength side of the hump; the printed peak is the end of the range.

What it shows

  • The sampled maximum is the end point of the range, not a peak of the curve.
  • The physical integral loses a large part of the spectrum, which the exact reference line reveals. The small step near 0.8 µm is the Wien switch at x = 3.
  • Lowering --x_max below 20 also needs a lower --x_high; --x_max 10 alone is rejected with the four values.

Headline values

  • Peak at x = 4.000000; peak wavelength 6.096512e-07 m.
  • Physical integral 1.291514e+07, 0.59 of the exact 2.187106e+07.
  • --x_max 10 --x_high 9: area 114.030238, physical integral 2.166218e+07, peak x = 4.965040.
  • --x_min 1 --x_low 1: area 121.952341, physical integral 2.111337e+07.
  • --y_frac_window 0: same numbers as the default run; the plot shows the whole computed range.

Notes for evaluation

  • Radiance, not flux: the physical integral of the two radiance quantities is in W m−2 sr−1; the emitted flux is π times it. The energy-density integral is stored energy per volume.
  • Peaks: the printed peak is the largest grid sample, within one grid step of the true peak only for the exact curve on a range that contains it (Beats 6 to 8 show the exceptions).
  • Display: the default --y_frac_window 0.003 limits only the plotted horizontal range; the vertical axis starts at zero. Neither affects the printed numbers.
  • Verification: the regression suite runs every figure command above and checks that every number quoted in this guide is printed by the program.
