Major Improvements in This Release

  • Massless test particles (Kickoff5). Particles feel every massive body and pull on none, so each costs one force term per massive body. New options --test_positions_init, --test_velocities_init, --test_ring, --test_ring_per_radius, --test_phases (golden-angle or random), --test_seed, --test_center, --removal_radii, --escape_radius and --test_csv. Particles starting inside a removal radius are removed at once. Existing commands and the massive-body results are unchanged.
  • Survival output (Kickoff5). --output_type survival keeps no paths and records each particle's fate (survived, encounter, escaped, numerical) and removal time, with a per-particle console table. Two windows follow: survival time and fate by starting position (removed particles red, horseshoes yellow, other survivors blue and green), then the end positions of the tadpole and circulating survivors, flat and seen from above turning with the reference body.
  • Help (Kickoff5 and later). New Beat 9 on Jupiter clearing its neighbourhood and a Trojan search; Experiments 11 and 12 (planets around a binary and around one star of a binary). The owner added the N2 cost, tree-code and test-particle paragraphs to "Reading the output" (coverage items MU3 and MU4).
  • Per-particle step control (Response-to-Audit51 and 52). Each particle's step is halved, down to 1/65536 of the massive-body step, until it is finite, converged, passes eps1 and does not cross a removal radius; a particle that cannot is given the fate numerical. This changed printed survival times and fates relative to Kickoff5; the Help quotes the current values.
  • Angle-path names (Response-to-Audit51 to 55). Survivors are named tadpole L4/L5, horseshoe, circulating, passed body or unfinished. Tadpole and horseshoe names need a completed swing of dlambda, the particle's mean longitude minus the reference body's, printed as a range in the table. L4/L5 markers are correct for equal-mass binaries, and L4/L5 names for clockwise systems.
  • Cancellation scale (Response-to-Audit53). Where the pulls on a particle nearly cancel, eps1 is judged against a tenth of the sum of the separate pulls.
  • Mean-longitude domain (Response-to-Audit54 and 55). dlambda is computed from the full three-dimensional orbit, only for orbits within 5° of the x-y plane going round the same way as the reference body; other particles are integrated but get no dlambda and no tadpole or horseshoe name. This changes printed values only for particles off the x-y plane; every Help command is planar and unchanged.
  • Sample Outputs Guide. All figures regenerated under build da455c3a0668, with two new Beat 9 figures (eleven in all) and working contents links.

Known Limitations

  • Direct pairwise Newtonian point-mass gravity, with no collision or softening model, tides, gas, radiation or relativity. Exact overlap of massive bodies is singular.
  • The adaptive predictor–corrector is not symplectic. Check conservation drift and compare runs at a matched simulated time.
  • COM display translates the plot only; integration and printed totals use input coordinates. Animation memory includes a per-step conservation history not capped by the one-million-frame guard.
  • Six default plotting colours, repeating with more bodies. MathJax needs an online CDN.
  • Plots open interactively; there is no built-in figure-saving option (--test_csv covers survival data).
  • BUILD_ID covers the four Python modules only.
  • Test-particle step control reduces close-approach error but does not bound error accumulated over a run; runs with many close approaches are slower.
  • Where pulls cancel to under a tenth of their sum, the step error there is the trapezoidal rule's ordinary second-order error.
  • At the deepest substep a straight-line crossing of a removal radius is taken as the encounter; a curved path entering the radius between substep points can still be missed.
  • Escape is a distance test from the massive-body centre of mass; a bound particle on a wide excursion counts as escaped. Encounter is a close-approach cut, not a collision model.
  • a0 and e0 treat the centre as a point mass and ignore the other bodies.
  • --test_ring makes x-y-plane, prograde orbits only; angles and ending distances are measured in the x-y plane from one reference body.
  • Co-orbital names describe the run made; a longer run can change them. They are given only for a planar, nearly circular pair (e ≤ 0.1, tilt ≤ 5°), a ring about its centre of mass, a reference body below 3.85% of the mass, and particle orbits within 5° of the x-y plane going round the same way.
  • Quasi-satellite motion is not named; it is reported as circulating, passed body or unfinished.
  • With only the Sun and Jupiter, horseshoe orbits are long lived; survival alone does not separate them from Trojans.
  • Animation keeps substep states for the current massive-body step only; heavily refined particles make frame generation slower.
  • The console uses days for the massive-body summary and AU and years for the test particles.

Open Bugs

  • M-1. Severity: P2. Finding: Codex Audit34 item 1.
    Symptom: the Beats Help's claim that 'animation' always stores far fewer position and velocity states than 'trajectories' does not hold when frame_time is short relative to the accepted-step spacing; one accepted step can generate many interpolated frames.
    Reproduction: with the default three-body initial state, --dt 2000 --max_steps 20 --frame_time 100, both output modes accept 20 steps. Observed: 'trajectories' stores 21 position and 21 velocity states; 'animation' stores 401 interpolated frames, more than 'trajectories', and still passes the 1,000,000-frame guard. Expected (per the Help's current wording): animation storage smaller than trajectories' in every case.
  • M-2. Severity: P3. Finding: Codex Audit34 item 2.
    Symptom: plot_trajectories() and animate_multiple() accept a hand-built result dictionary whose position array has a zero-body dimension instead of rejecting it with a clear shape error before constructing a figure. Normal run_simulation() output always has at least two bodies and is unaffected.
    Reproduction: plot_trajectories({'type':'trajectories', 'positions':np.empty((1,0,3)),'display_frame':'user'}). Observed: an empty plot after a Matplotlib "No artists with labels" warning. Expected: a clear ValueError naming the invalid shape. Similarly, animate_multiple() with a matching zero-body frame_positions does raise ValueError, but with NumPy's raw internal message, "zero-size array to reduction operation minimum which has no identity," raised from _fixed_limits(), instead of a message that names the invalid shape the way every other validated case in this module does.

Major Improvements in This Release

  • Kickoff (1.4.0, build f166d2b70ae6): new tutorial Help file. Added Multiple-extended.html, a Beats-format tutorial covering the program end to end with runnable commands and printed values checked against the build, alongside the existing classic Multiple.html.
  • Kickoff (1.4.0): scale-aware near-cancellation fallback. Momentum and angular-momentum drift normalization now falls back to a scale-aware characteristic value when the initial vector is close to, but not exactly, zero; previously only an exact-zero initial vector used that fallback. This changes which drift-normalization label a near-cancelling run prints, not the underlying integration or any already-correct drift value.
  • Response-to-Audit31 (build d926f8b8be68): plotting input validation. animate_multiple() now rejects a malformed scalar or None frame_positions with a clear ValueError instead of a bare IndexError.
  • Response-to-Audit31: Help-file corrections and Windows quoting. Corrected four documentation overclaims in the Beats Help (the Beat 8 convergence-comparison methodology, the Beat 7 frame-count example, the Beat 0 drift-vs-rounding claim, and the Beat 2 boost/translation-invariance wording); relabeled Eq. 12 the "constant-acceleration predictor"; changed every semicolon-separated position/velocity triple in both Help files from single to double quotes so the shown commands work in cmd.exe as well as a POSIX shell.
  • Response-to-Audit32 (build 412f37f02fdc): trial-step singularity retry. A predictor or corrector trial configuration that lands two bodies on exactly the same point now halves the working timestep and retries, the same recovery already used for a non-finite trial acceleration, instead of aborting the run. A genuinely coincident initial or accepted state still raises immediately.
  • Response-to-Audit32: main.py Windows quoting. The module docstring's example and the --positions_init/ --velocities_init metavars now use double quotes, so python main.py --help prints a copy-pasteable example on Windows as well as POSIX shells.
  • Response-to-Audit33 (Help-file wording only; build unchanged): corrected the Beats Help's description of animation memory use (both output modes keep one conservation-diagnostic record per accepted step; only the stored frame count is bounded by frame_time and the 1,000,000-frame guard) and removed an unsupported claim about why one finer-timestep run in Beat 8 showed a larger window-maximum energy drift than a coarser one.

Known Limitations

  • Intended for education rather than research-grade N-body work: direct all-pairs Newtonian point-mass gravity only, with no softening, collisions, tides, gas, radiation, or relativity.
  • Direct pairwise force evaluation scales as O(N2), intended for modest educational body counts.
  • The predictor-corrector integrator is not symplectic. Close encounters and long integrations should always be checked with the printed conservation drift and with convergence tests that compare runs over the same simulated time interval.
  • Exact coincidence of two bodies is a genuine physical singularity for a point-mass force law and terminates the run with a clear error; this is correct behavior, not a defect.
  • display_frame='com' and 'user' are display-only translations. Integration and the printed E, P, and L totals are always computed in the user-input inertial frame.
  • Both output modes retain one energy/momentum/angular-momentum record per accepted step for the run's whole duration, in addition to whichever position/velocity data the chosen output_type stores. Only the stored animation frame count is bounded, by frame_time and the 1,000,000-frame guard; the per-accepted-step conservation history is not bounded by either (see Open Bug M-1 for a related, still-inaccurate memory claim in the Help text).
  • Only six plotting colors are supplied by default; colors repeat for a simulation with more than six bodies.
  • Both Help files load MathJax from a public CDN. Offline, equations appear as raw LaTeX/TeX rather than typeset mathematics.
  • BUILD_ID is computed from physics_multiple.py, driver_multiple.py, main.py, and plot_multiple.py only. Help-only or test-only edits do not change it.

Known Minor Maintenance Items

  • Which Help file the regression suite's Help-parsing tests scrape, Multiple.html or Multiple-extended.html, is undecided (Grok, policy).
  • Whether students are given the long Beats Help file or a shorter cut is undecided (Grok, policy).
  • The classic Help's cross-reference to a Binary "original.html" filename has not been independently checked for fragility (Grok Audit33/34, observation).
  • A softening length or launch-offset option for the galaxy-collision toy scenario, and more than six default plotting colors, remain unimplemented optional enhancements (Grok, optional later).
  • The Output Modes reference section's own text still points to "Beat 7, Output Modes above" from a sentence that is itself inside the Output Modes section; a harmless circular cross-reference (Grok Audit34, cosmetic).
