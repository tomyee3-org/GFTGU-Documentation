What the program calculates

MercPert models the planar circular restricted three-body problem. The Sun and a companion follow prescribed circular orbits about their barycentre, while “Mercury” is a negligible-mass test particle whose trajectory responds to both bodies without affecting either of them.

Initial test-particle positions and velocities are entered relative to the Sun, then converted to barycentric inertial coordinates. In the orbit plots, the Sun and companion are dashed red and green reference curves; the integrated test-particle path is solid blue. Filled circles mark starting positions.

A crossing of two plotted curves does not by itself mean that the objects occupied that point at the same time. For runs longer than one binary period, the plotting routine deliberately shows only one complete reference orbit for each massive body while retaining the test particle’s full trajectory.

Figure 1 — Default strong-companion case

binary = BinarySystemParams(
    m_sun_solar=1.0,
    m_planet_solar=0.1,
    binary_separation=0.7 * AU)
mercury = MercuryInitialConditions(
    x_init=0.3 * AU, y_init=0.0,
    vx_init=0.0, vy_init=59220.0)
run = MercPertRunParams(
    dt=2000.0, max_steps=10000,
    eps1=0.05, eps2=0.0001,
    sun_collision_radius=R_SUN)

The default companion is deliberately extreme: 0.1 solar masses, roughly one hundred Jupiter masses and therefore a low-mass star rather than a planet.

What it shows

  • The massive pair’s prescribed circular period is 203.96 days; the test particle was integrated for 231.48 days.
  • The test particle’s distance from the Sun ranges from 0.300 to 0.408 AU over the sampled interval.
  • The run reaches its 10,000-step limit without a configured collision; the maximum fractional Jacobi drift is 1.230e-06.

Companion mass: 0.1 M☉  |  Integration: 231.48 days  |  Termination: max_steps reached

binary = BinarySystemParams(

Reducing the companion to a Jupiter-like mass and using the approximate isolated circular speed at 0.3 AU produces a much quieter baseline orbit.

What it shows

  • The test particle remains very close to 0.3 AU from the Sun: its sampled range is 0.299929–0.300352 AU.
  • The binary period becomes 213.82 days because the total mass is slightly smaller than in Figure 1.
  • The maximum fractional Jacobi drift is 1.222e-07. The contrast with Figure 1 isolates the physical effect of companion mass as well as the changed starting speed.

Figure 4 — Jacobi-constant diagnostic

# Diagnostic for the Figure 3 integration
plot_jacobi_drift(output)

In the ideal circular restricted three-body model, the Jacobi constant is conserved. Its numerical drift is therefore an accuracy diagnostic, not a new physical effect.

What it shows

  • The largest absolute fractional drift during the extended run is 5.162e-03, or 0.516%.
  • The growing excursions coincide with the more demanding late-time evolution and warn that conclusions should be checked with smaller dt and tighter tolerances.
  • Because perturbed three-body trajectories can be sensitive, visibly different late paths do not alone prove numerical failure; convergence over a common time interval is the stronger test.

Maximum fractional drift: 5.162e-03  |  Recommended follow-up: repeat with stricter numerical controls

Notes for evaluation

  • Model scope: this is a planar circular restricted three-body model. The two massive bodies follow prescribed circular motion; the test particle has no back-reaction.
  • Numerical method: an Euler predictor is followed by an iterated trapezoidal corrector. The working timestep is reduced when the predicted acceleration changes too much or the corrector fails to converge.
  • Collision handling: configurable moving circular boundaries can stop the trajectory at the Sun or companion. The default enables the nominal solar radius and disables a companion radius because the companion’s physical nature is user-selectable.
  • Termination: the driver stops at max_steps unless a configured collision occurs. Escape is an interpretation made from the trajectory over a stated finite interval, not a separate automatic stop condition.
  • Suggested investigations: the documentation develops companion-mass sequences, prograde/retrograde comparisons, close encounters, convergence tests, finite-time stability maps, circumbinary motion, and an L1 test-particle experiment.
  • Build verification: the executable and documentation both identify version 1.0.2, build 9ae1fff767c9. Following the economical preparation workflow used for this guide series, no regression suite was run.
