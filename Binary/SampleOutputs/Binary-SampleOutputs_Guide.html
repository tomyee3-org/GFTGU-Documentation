Binary

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.2.4  |  Build 53b5e2037ced  |  Updated 2026-09-26

Purpose. These eight figures, one for each Beat of the Help file, give an instructor a compact visual survey of what Binary computes: two Newtonian point masses moving under their mutual gravity, integrated step by step, with each run printing a summary and showing one plot. Where a Beat uses several commands, the figure shows one of them and the headline values also cover the others. For Beats 5 and 7 the plot looks the same as the default run; the evidence for those Beats is in the printed energy rows quoted with the figures.

Beat 0 — The default equal-mass binary

python main.py

The default run: two bodies of 2×1030 kg start 9.2×1010 m apart on the x axis with equal and opposite sideways speeds of 13000 m/s, and trace mirror-image ellipses about their common centre of mass.

What it shows

  • The only force is the mutual gravitational pull along the line joining the bodies, and it is enough to bend both paths into closed ellipses.
  • The bodies move slowly when far apart and fast when close, and the two tracks are exact mirror images because the masses are equal.
  • The run stops by itself when the relative position has turned through one full revolution.

Headline values

  • 2284 accepted steps of 2000 s, one relative revolution, 52.87 days; separations from 1.2127e+10 m (minimum, sampled) to 9.2e+10 m.
  • Initial Keplerian orbit elliptic, eccentricity 0.76705, semi-major axis 2.6032e+10 m and period 52.874 days for each body.
  • Periapsis 6.0643e+09 m at 98610 m/s and apoapsis 4.6e+10 m at 13000 m/s.
  • Initial total energy −2.5639e+39 J; the printed departure (E−E0)/|E0| is −1.2814e-03 in row 0.5 and −3.1646e-06 in row 1.0.

Beat 1 — Unequal masses on circles

python main.py --MA 2e30 --MB 1e30 --xInitA 3.0666666667e10 --xInitB -6.1333333333e10 --uInitA 15550.5265 --uInitB -31101.053

A 2:1 mass ratio started on circular orbits: the heavier body A moves on the smaller circle and the lighter body B on a circle twice as large, both centred on the centre of mass at the origin.

What it shows

  • The two bodies stay on opposite sides of the centre of mass, each at a distance in inverse proportion to its mass.
  • Both go round in the same time, so the lighter body must move twice as fast.
  • Neither body is at rest: the centre of mass, not either body, is the fixed point.

Headline values

  • Radii 3.0666e+10 m and 6.1332e+10 m for A and B, a ratio of 2.0000, adding up to the 9.2e+10 m separation.
  • Speeds 15551 m/s for A and 31102 m/s for B; period 143.41 days for both.
  • 6196 accepted steps, 1.0001 revolutions, 143.43 days; initial eccentricity 1.4843e-05, an almost exact circle.
  • Initial total energy −7.2548e+38 J, almost exactly minus the kinetic energy 7.2546e+38 J, as for a circular orbit.

Beat 2 — A boosted pair

python main.py --vInitA 60000 --vInitB 60000

The default pair given a common velocity of 60000 m/s along x: the centre of mass drifts steadily to the right and each body traces a wavy, drifting path instead of a closed ellipse. The other Beat 2 command, which shifts the starting positions, gives a figure of the default shape displaced from the origin.

What it shows

  • A common velocity added to both bodies changes the paths that are drawn but not the relative motion of the two bodies.
  • The centre of mass moves in a straight line at constant speed, so the tracks are shifted and stretched, not closed.
  • The printed separations, step count and eccentricity are those of the default run; the inertial-frame energy is not.

Headline values

  • Steps 2284, 52.87 days, separations 1.2127e+10 m to 9.2e+10 m and eccentricity 0.76705, exactly as for the default run.
  • The centre of mass moves 60000 m/s × 4.568e+06 s ≈ 2.74×1011 m in the run.
  • Initial total energy +4.6361e+39 J against −2.5639e+39 J for the default, because the kinetic energy now includes the drift of the centre of mass (7.538e+39 J against 3.38e+38 J).
  • The shifted-start command (--xInitA 5e10 --xInitB -4.2e10 --yInitA 1e10 --yInitB 1e10) prints the same steps, separations and energy rows as the default run.

Beat 3 — The energy plot for the default orbit

python main.py --output_type energy_vs_time

Kinetic energy, potential energy and their sum against time for the default run: the two parts swap large amounts of energy at closest approach while the total stays flat and negative.

What it shows

  • The total is negative, which is what it means for the pair to be bound and to come back.
  • Kinetic energy peaks and potential energy dips at the same moment, at closest approach half way through the run.
  • Starting faster changes the sign of the total: a run started at 38091 m/s has a total energy near zero, and a faster one is unbound.

Headline values

  • Initial potential −2.9019e+39 J, kinetic 3.38e+38 J, total −2.5639e+39 J; at row 0.5 (26.435 days) the potential is −2.2015e+40 J and the kinetic 1.9448e+40 J.
  • Speed 38091.14 m/s each (with --no-stop_after_one_orbit --max_steps 1000): total energy +3.2778e+32 J, printed as “hyperbolic; eccentricity: 1”; runs to max_steps in 23.148 days, and (E−E0)/|E0| reaches 4.0631 because E0 is so small.
  • Speed 60000 m/s each: total energy +4.2981e+39 J, hyperbolic with eccentricity 3.9623, no period; the separation reaches 2.2819e+11 m in 23.148 days.

Beat 4 — Two circles in velocity space

python main.py --output_type velocity_space

The velocity-space plot for the default run: each body's velocity vector traces a circle, and the origin lies inside both circles. The other Beat 4 command, the circular start, gives a single circle centred on the origin (the two bodies’ circles coincide).

What it shows

  • The velocity of a Kepler orbit traces a circle, whatever the shape of the orbit in space.
  • The distance from the origin to the circle's centre, divided by the circle's radius, is the eccentricity.
  • With the centre of mass at rest, the origin lies inside the circle for a bound orbit; a circular orbit gives a circle centred on the origin.

Headline values

  • Each circle has radius 55805 m/s, half of 98610 + 13000, and its centre is 42805 m/s from the origin, half of 98610 − 13000; 42805/55805 = 0.76705.
  • Speeds 98610 m/s at periapsis and 13000 m/s at apoapsis, whose ratio 7.585 equals the ratio of the distances 4.6e+10 / 6.0643e+09.
  • The products of distance and speed at the two apsides are both 5.980×1014 m2 s−1 for body A.
  • Circular start (--uInitA 26934.5 --uInitB -26934.5): speeds 26935 and 26934 m/s, eccentricity 1.393e-07, 5366 steps, 124.21 days, 1.0001 revolutions.

Beat 5 — A very loose corrector

python main.py --eps2 0.5

The default run with the corrector allowed to stop at a 50% change instead of 0.01%; at this scale the plot cannot be told from the Beat 0 figure, and the difference is in the printed energy rows.

What it shows

  • The corrector repeats its trapezoidal update until successive results agree to within eps2, so a looser eps2 means less refinement per step.
  • A looser eps2 leaves the orbit's shape intact to the eye; the cost shows up in the energy table, not the plot.
  • A tighter eps2 lowers the energy error at the end of the run, where the loose-corrector error is left over.

Headline values

  • --eps2 0.5: 2285 steps, 52.894 days; (E−E0)/|E0| is −1.784e-03 in row 0.5 and +8.1292e-05 in row 1.0.
  • Default (eps2 = 1e-4): 2284 steps, −1.2814e-03 in row 0.5 and −3.1646e-06 in row 1.0.
  • --eps2 1e-8: 2284 steps, −1.1854e-03 in row 0.5 and 3.2091e-12 in row 1.0.

Beat 6 — A step control that is too loose

python main.py --dt 20000 --eps1 0.5

A ten-times-larger timestep with the step control loosened to 50%: the two tracks are visibly distorted and no longer mirror images. The other Beat 6 command, --dt 20000 with the default eps1, keeps them nearly symmetric.

What it shows

  • The step control shortens the working step when the predicted acceleration changes too much between its start and end, which happens near closest approach.
  • With eps1 loosened to 0.5 the step is never shortened in this run (222 steps of 20000 s make 51.389 days), so the coarse step goes through closest approach unresolved.
  • The result is an orbit that still turns once round but has the wrong shape, which the energy table also shows.

Headline values

  • --dt 20000 --eps1 0.5: 222 steps, 51.389 days, 1.0007 revolutions, minimum separation 1.194e+10 m; (E−E0)/|E0| is −4.0345e-02 in row 0.5.
  • --dt 20000 with default eps1: 387 steps, 52.984 days, minimum separation 1.2113e+10 m, −2.2712e-03 in row 0.5 and −2.775e-05 in row 1.0.
  • For comparison the default run (dt 2000) takes 2284 steps and has −1.2814e-03 in row 0.5.

Beat 7 — Halving the timestep

python main.py --dt 1000 --eps2 1e-8

A run with half the default timestep and a very tight corrector; as in Beat 5 the plot cannot be told from the default figure, and the evidence for accuracy is in the printed energy rows.

What it shows

  • Repeating a run with a smaller timestep and a tighter tolerance is the way to see how much of the answer to trust.
  • Halving dt cuts the energy dip at closest approach by about a factor of four, which is the behaviour of a second-order method.
  • With the corrector tight, the end-of-run residual all but vanishes, so what remains is the dip from the finite step.

Headline values

  • --dt 1000 --eps2 1e-8: 4568 steps; −2.9623e-04 in row 0.5 and −9.4305e-15 in row 1.0.
  • --dt 1000: 4569 steps; −4.5152e-04 in row 0.5 and +1.0168e-05 in row 1.0.
  • --dt 500: 9137 steps; −1.1354e-04 in row 0.5 (a factor of 3.98 below the dt 1000 value) and +1.2713e-06 in row 1.0.
  • The default (dt 2000) gives −1.2814e-03 in row 0.5.

Notes for evaluation

  • The model treats two point masses under Newtonian gravity only. Its results illustrate the equations; they are not an ephemeris.
  • Every run here stops after one relative revolution, except the two unbound runs of Beat 3, which stop at max_steps.
  • The energy departure is one diagnostic of the numerical error; it does not by itself bound the error in positions, period or phase. The printed table has eleven interpolated rows, so the largest departure can fall between them.
  • Every headline value was read from the summary printed by the same build, using the commands shown; the Help gives the derivations.
