Random2

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.4.0  |  Build 06ebd8fea601  |  Updated 24 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide is for an instructor who has the Random2 Help (Random2-claude.html) open. It shows one figure for each Beat of the Help, made with a command from that Beat, together with the values the run prints. It is not a retelling of the tutorial. No external image files are required.

Random2 runs three-dimensional random walks to show that the distance travelled grows as √N (scaled_distance), and draws fixed-length, direction-random walks escaping from a circular schematic star (walk2d). Every run prints a summary and then opens its plot. Every command below includes --seed, so it reproduces exactly the figure and numbers shown.

Beat 0 — Random steps add up as √N

python main.py --seed 12

The default experiment: 100 three-dimensional walks for each step count from 2 to 4096, on axes with equal decades.

What it shows

  • The points lie close to a straight line of slope 1/2: four times as many steps take the walker about twice as far.
  • The ratio of the mean distance to √N stays near the large-N prediction for every step count.
  • The printed standard errors set the scatter of the points about the line.

Headline values

  • N = 4096: mean scaled distance 63.0073, standard error 2.3473, ratio to sqrt(N) 0.9845.
  • N = 1024: mean scaled distance 30.6802, ratio 0.9588.
  • Large-N prediction for the ratio 0.9591; fitted log-log slope 0.4964.

Beat 1 — How far to trust an average

python main.py --seed 12 --n_trials 1000

The same experiment with ten times as many trials: the points hug the line much more closely.

What it shows

  • Ten times as many trials shrink the standard errors by about √10.
  • From 16 steps on, every ratio to √N is within 0.008 of the large-N prediction.
  • The fitted slope hardly changes with the number of trials; a single row's standard error says more about its accuracy.

Headline values

  • N = 4096: mean 61.0296, standard error 0.8231 (100 trials: 2.3473; 25 trials: 4.2617).
  • N = 16: standard error 0.0501, so the standard error grows about as √N.
  • Fitted slopes: 0.4998 (25 trials), 0.4964 (100), 0.4969 (1000).

Beat 2 — Only the variance matters

python main.py --seed 12 --step_distribution gaussian

Gaussian instead of uniform step components: the exponent is unchanged and only the constant moves.

What it shows

  • The slope is still close to 1/2; the square-root law does not depend on the shape of the step distribution.
  • The large-N prediction for the ratio to √N becomes 1 instead of 0.9591.
  • The last rows of the two runs differ by less than their combined standard error.

Headline values

  • Large-N prediction 1.0000; fitted log-log slope 0.4986.
  • N = 4096: mean 65.1655, standard error 3.2761, ratio 1.0182.
  • Uniform steps (Beat 0) at N = 4096: mean 63.0073, standard error 2.3473.

Beat 3 — A photon in a schematic star

python main.py --display walk2d --seed 12

Four fixed-length, direction-random walks from the centre of the default star, each stopped at its exact exit point.

What it shows

  • Each path wanders back and forth many times before it happens to reach the edge.
  • The four escape step counts differ by a factor of almost five.
  • The default radius is twice the distance a 2000-step walk typically covers.

Headline values

  • Star radius R 89.4427; (R / mean free path)^2 8000.0.
  • Escape step counts 11564, 7519, 8246 and 2479.
  • Mean 7452.0 (standard error 1876.9), median 7882.5.

Beat 4 — Escape takes (R/λ)² steps

python main.py --display walk2d --radius 30 --n_walks 200 --seed 12

Two hundred walks from the centre of a star of radius 30: enough to measure the mean escape time.

What it shows

  • The mean escape step count agrees with (R/λ)² to within its standard error.
  • The distribution of escape times is broad, with a long tail that pulls the mean above the median.
  • Rays leave in all directions, as expected for an isotropic walk.

Headline values

  • (R / mean free path)^2 900.0; 200 of 200 escaped.
  • Mean 902.7 (standard error 44.7); ratio to (R/λ)² 1.0030.
  • Median 742.5; fastest 132 steps, slowest 3797.

Beat 5 — Twice the radius, four times the steps

python main.py --display walk2d --radius 15 --n_walks 200 --seed 12

The run of Beat 4 in a star of half the radius.

What it shows

  • The mean escape step count falls by a factor close to 4, not 2.
  • The medians fall by the same factor.
  • The ratio lies within the range that the escape-time bound allows for these small stars.

Headline values

  • (R / mean free path)^2 225.0; mean 227.8 (standard error 10.5).
  • Ratio of means, radius 30 over radius 15: 3.96; ratio of medians 3.99.
  • Median 186.0; fastest 34 steps, slowest 787.

Beat 6 — Only R/λ matters

python main.py --display walk2d --mean_free_path 2 --seed 12

The default star with every step twice as long: the default radius doubles with it, and the picture is the Beat 3 figure at twice the scale.

What it shows

  • With the same seed the walks are the same walks, drawn at a different scale.
  • Every escape step count is the same as in Beat 3.
  • (R/λ)² is unchanged, because the default radius is proportional to the step length.

Headline values

  • Star radius R 178.8854; mean free path 2; (R / mean free path)^2 8000.0.
  • Escape step counts 11564, 7519, 8246 and 2479, the same as with a step length of 1.
  • Mean escape steps / (R / mean free path)^2: 0.9315, as in Beat 3.

Beat 7 — The step cap

python main.py --display walk2d --radius 30 --n_walks 200 --step_cap 900 --seed 12

The radius-30 run of Beat 4 with each walk stopped after 900 steps; stopped walks end in an x.

What it shows

  • Many walks are stopped before they escape and are left out of the escape statistics.
  • The mean over the walks that did escape is far below (R/λ)²: a censored, low-biased sample.
  • Raising the cap to 3000 stops only a few walks but still lowers the mean.

Headline values

  • Escaped walks 130 of 200; mean 515.7, ratio to (R/λ)² 0.5730; slowest counted 897.
  • --step_cap 3000: escaped 196 of 200; mean 837.5, ratio 0.9305.
  • Without an effective cap (Beat 4): mean 902.7, ratio 1.0030.

Beat 8 — Seeds and the hard cases

python main.py --seed 28

The default experiment of Beat 0 with a different seed: a new random sample of the same law.

What it shows

  • The points differ in detail from the seed-12 plot but follow the same line.
  • The difference between the two last rows is small compared with their combined standard error.
  • Without --seed every run differs; --max_steps 1 is rejected, because the smallest walk has 2 steps.

Headline values

  • N = 4096: mean 62.2140, standard error 2.5542 (seed 12: 63.0073, standard error 2.3473).
  • Fitted log-log slope 0.4974 (seed 12: 0.4964).
  • Difference 0.79, against a combined standard error of 3.47.

Notes for evaluation

  • Monte Carlo: without --seed every run is a new random sample; the standard errors in the summary say how much its numbers should vary.
  • Large N: the √N law and its constant are large-N results; the shortest walks have a ratio to √N a little above the prediction.
  • Escape time: the mean escape step count lies between (R/λ)² and (R/λ + 1)²; walks stopped by the step cap are excluded, which biases the mean low.
  • Scope: the star is a two-dimensional picture of diffusion, not a model of the Sun's opacity or convection.
  • Verification: the regression suite runs every figure command above and checks that every number quoted in this guide is printed by the program.
