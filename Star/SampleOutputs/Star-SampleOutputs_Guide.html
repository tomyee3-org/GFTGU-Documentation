Star

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.2.0  |  Build 7d6e2d9d542c  |  Updated 25 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide is for an instructor who has the Star Help (Star-claude.html) open. It shows one figure for each of the nine Beats of the Help, made with that Beat's command, together with the values the run prints. Where a Beat has several commands, the others are covered in its headline values. No external image files are required.

Star integrates Newtonian hydrostatic equilibrium and mass continuity outward from the centre of a star whose pressure and density are linked by one power law, the polytrope, and plots its pressure, density, temperature or enclosed mass against radius. Every run also prints the radius and mass, the radial step and any restarts, the Lane–Emden radius and mass of the same polytrope as a numerical reference with the relative differences of the integrated values, and five interior checkpoints. The default inputs give about the Sun's radius and mass, but the interior is a teaching model, not the Sun's.

Beat 0 — A star that holds itself up

python main.py

The pressure of the default star falls from 7.158e+15 Pa at the centre to zero at the surface, 6.9674e+08 m out.

What it shows

  • The curve is flat at the centre, where the enclosed mass and so the pull are zero, and falls fastest a little way out.
  • Beyond about half the radius the pressure is below 1% of its central value, so on this linear axis the outer half looks flat.
  • The curve ends at the interpolated surface point, where the pressure is exactly zero.

Headline values

  • Radius 6.9674e+08 m; total mass 1.9825e+30 kg.
  • Numerical grid: radial step 5.2645e+05 m, 1325 grid points, 0 restarts.
  • Lane–Emden reference: n = 2.7778, radius 7.0676e+08 m, mass 1.9861e+30 kg; integrated radius differs by -0.014174, mass by -0.0018494.
  • Pressure 1.9997e+15 Pa at 0.25R (0.279 of central) and 1.12e+14 Pa at 0.5R (0.0156 of central).

Beat 1 — What the pressure needs: a density

python main.py --output_type density --log_y

On a logarithmic axis the density falls smoothly from 49187 kg/m³ at the centre and then plunges in the last few percent of the radius.

What it shows

  • The density follows the pressure through the polytrope, so it falls in the same way, but less steeply.
  • The logarithmic axis shows the thin outer envelope that a linear axis squeezes onto zero.
  • The exact zero at the surface cannot be drawn on a logarithmic axis and is left out.

Headline values

  • Central density 49187 kg/m³; the ideal gas law with p_c = 7.158e+15 Pa, T_c = 2.263e+07 K and μ = 1.285 gives 49186.7.
  • Density 19258 kg/m³ at 0.25R, 2313.2 at 0.5R, 128.6 at 0.75R and 6.6545 at 0.9R.
  • Lowest plotted value 2.45e-05 kg/m³, at the last grid point, 6.965e+08 m.
  • Radius, mass and grid as in Beat 0.

Beat 2 — The temperature comes last

python main.py --output_type temperature

The temperature, computed from the pressure and density by the ideal gas law, falls from 2.263e+07 K at the centre to zero at the surface.

What it shows

  • The temperature falls much more gently than the pressure or the density: it is still a third of its central value at half the radius.
  • No heat flow is computed; the temperature follows from the pressure and density alone.
  • The zero at the surface belongs to the model, not to a real star.

Headline values

  • Central temperature 2.263e+07 K.
  • 1.6147e+07 K at 0.25R, 7.5289e+06 K at 0.5R, 2.6604e+06 K at 0.75R, 9.1609e+05 K at 0.9R.
  • The temperature halves at about 0.38R in the stored profile.

Beat 3 — Where the mass is

python main.py --output_type mass

The enclosed mass rises from zero at the centre to 1.9825e+30 kg, and levels off in the outer layers.

What it shows

  • The mass grows slowly at first, because the volume near the centre is small, and fastest at about 0.3 of the radius.
  • Half of the mass lies inside about a third of the radius; the outer quarter holds only about 1%.
  • The star is centrally condensed: the central density is 35 times the mean.

Headline values

  • Mass 6.2826e+29 kg inside 0.25R, 1.6732e+30 kg inside 0.5R (84.4%), 1.9625e+30 kg inside 0.75R (99.0%), 1.9818e+30 kg inside 0.9R.
  • Mean density from the printed radius and mass: 1399 kg/m³.
  • Central to mean density: 35.2 from the integrated star; 36.62 from the Lane–Emden solution (Beat 5).

Beat 4 — One star, a family of stars

python main.py --T_c 2.4893e7

With the central temperature raised by 10% and the central pressure unchanged, the pressure curve has the same shape, stretched by 1.1 in radius.

What it shows

  • The star is the same dimensionless polytrope, scaled: the pressure at each fraction of the radius is unchanged.
  • The radius grows by 1.1 and the mass by 1.21, the square of 1.1.
  • The Euler steps land on the same points of the same dimensionless star, so the relative errors are those of Beat 0.

Headline values

  • Radius 7.6641e+08 m (1.1000 × default); total mass 2.3988e+30 kg (1.2100 × default).
  • Radial step 5.791e+05 m, 1325 grid points, 0 restarts.
  • Central density 44715 kg/m³; pressure 1.12e+14 Pa at 0.5R, as in Beat 0.
  • Lane–Emden radius 7.7743e+08 m, mass 2.4032e+30 kg; differences -0.014174 and -0.0018494, as in Beat 0.

Beat 5 — The Lane–Emden solution

python main.py --gamma 2

With γ = 2 (index n = 1) the star is much smaller, 2.6239e+08 m, and the pressure falls smoothly to its surface without a long tail.

What it shows

  • For n = 1 the Lane–Emden equation has a formula for its solution, so the printed Lane–Emden radius can be checked by hand.
  • The integrated radius is 0.6% short of the Lane–Emden radius, and the integrated mass 0.45% high.
  • The pressure falls much more gradually in the outer layers than in the default star.

Headline values

  • Polytropic index n = 1; 500 grid points, 0 restarts.
  • Lane–Emden radius 2.6392e+08 m, equal to √(πp_c/(2G))/ρ_c computed by hand; Lane–Emden mass 1.1513e+30 kg.
  • Integrated radius 2.6239e+08 m and mass 1.1565e+30 kg; differences -0.0058198 and 0.004502.
  • For the default n = 2.7778 the program finds ξ₁ = 6.1212 and ω_n = 2.0876, a central to mean density ratio of 36.62.

Beat 6 — Euler steps and their error

python main.py --steps_per_scale 800 --max_points 4000

With the radial step halved the curve looks the same as in Beat 0; the change is in the printed differences from the Lane–Emden solution.

What it shows

  • Halving the step halves the mass error, as a first-order method should.
  • The radius error falls by a little less than half.
  • The finer run needs enough points to avoid a restart, which would double the step again.

Headline values

  • Radial step 2.6323e+05 m, 2666 grid points, 0 restarts.
  • Radius 7.0145e+08 m, total mass 1.9843e+30 kg.
  • Radius differs by -0.0075071, 1.888 times smaller than the default -0.014174.
  • Mass differs by -0.00092707, 1.995 times smaller than the default -0.0018494.

Beat 7 — The surface and the restart

python main.py --max_points 1000

With too few points for the default step the program doubles the step once and restarts; the star comes out 2.7% short of the Lane–Emden radius.

What it shows

  • A restart doubles the radial step, which roughly doubles the errors.
  • Apart from the restart count, the run prints exactly what --steps_per_scale 200 prints.
  • The printed step and restart lines show when a comparison has been coarsened.

Headline values

  • Restarts 1; radial step 1.0529e+06 m; 654 grid points.
  • Radius 6.8749e+08 m, total mass 1.9788e+30 kg.
  • Radius differs by -0.02726 and mass by -0.0036786, 1.92 and 1.99 times the default errors.
  • python main.py --steps_per_scale 200: the same step, points, radius, mass and differences, with 0 restarts.

Beat 8 — Where one polytrope stops

python main.py --gamma 1.21

Close to γ = 6/5 the pressure collapses within the inner tenth of a star ten times larger than the default, and the surface lies in a vast, nearly empty envelope.

What it shows

  • Almost all of the mass lies in the core; the outer three quarters of the radius hold almost none.
  • Reaching the surface needs so many steps that the program restarts four times, and the integrated radius falls a quarter short.
  • Closer still to 6/5 the printed radius is an artefact, and the program says when its Lane–Emden values themselves become uncertain.

Headline values

  • n = 4.7619; 4 restarts, radial step 8.4233e+06 m (16 × default), 861 grid points.
  • Radius 7.2406e+09 m against the Lane–Emden 9.9626e+09 m (-0.27322); mass 2.8415e+30 kg against 3.0895e+30 kg (-0.080272).
  • Mass 2.8287e+30 kg inside 0.25R, 99.55% of the total.
  • python main.py --gamma 1.2000000001: radius 1.6874e+10 m against the Lane–Emden 1.0269e+18 m (difference -1); n printed as 4.9999999975; warning “uncertain by about 0.0001 (relative)”.
  • python main.py --gamma 1.2 is rejected with gamma must exceed 1.2 for a finite-radius polytrope; python main.py --output_type mass --log_y is rejected with --log_y cannot be used with --output_type mass because enclosed mass is zero at the center.

Notes for evaluation

  • Exponent and index: --gamma is the exponent γ of p ∝ ργ; the printed index is n = 1/(γ − 1), 2.7778 at the default γ = 1.36. γ must exceed 1.2.
  • Look-alike figures: the plots of Beats 0, 6 and 7 differ by about 1% in radius and look alike; the Beats are about the printed grid lines and Lane–Emden differences, not about the curves.
  • Restarts: check that Restarts (radial step doubled) is 0 before comparing a run with the Lane–Emden values; a restart doubles the step.
  • The Lane–Emden reference: it is computed numerically, twice, with the second step half the first. A warning line follows the Lane–Emden mass only within about 1.5e-7 of γ = 6/5, as in the second command of Beat 8.
