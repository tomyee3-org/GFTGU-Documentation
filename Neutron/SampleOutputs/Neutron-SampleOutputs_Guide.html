Figure 8 — Beat 8: the low-mass end of the equilibrium sequence

python main.py --pC 1e28 --output_type mass

A low central pressure produces a large, low-mass equilibrium without establishing a physical minimum neutron-star mass.

What it shows

  • Enclosed mass at central pressure 1e28 Pa.
  • A much larger radius and much smaller mass than the default equilibrium.
  • An extrapolation of the single polytrope into a regime where omitted crust and composition physics matter.

Headline values

  • Surface radius 54.713 km; total gravitational mass 0.020979 M_sun.
  • At pC=1e27 Pa: radius 68.909 km and mass 0.010529 M_sun, below the chapter’s simple 0.02-M_sun estimate.
  • At pC=1e34, 1e35 and 1e36 Pa: total masses 0.818445, 0.963016 and 0.849747 M_sun, respectively.
  • Near the turnover, pC=1e35, 1.1e35 and 1.2e35 Pa give masses 0.963016, 0.963197 and 0.962925 M_sun; the sampled peak is near 1.1e35 Pa.

Notes for evaluation

  • The default model, alternate exponents and central-pressure sweep describe mathematical equilibria of the chosen EOS; they are not measurements of real stars.
  • Figure 7 deliberately shows an acausal EOS configuration. Passing the Buchdahl check does not establish physical validity or dynamical stability.
  • The low-mass results in Figure 8 motivate the research question; they neither establish a physical minimum mass nor simulate formation.
  • The sampled turnover near 1.1e35 Pa corrects the older guide’s bracket between 1e35 and 1e36 Pa. The static program does not dynamically evolve the branch beyond the maximum.
  • The documented commands python main.py --gamma 1 and python main.py --max_steps 100 exit with errors before producing a completed model or figure, as expected.
