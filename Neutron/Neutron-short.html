Overview

Neutron computes static, spherically symmetric stellar equilibria in general relativity. It integrates the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) pressure equation and the enclosed-mass equation outward from the center until the pressure reaches zero, using one simple polytropic equation of state, \(p=K\rho^\gamma\). It is introduced in Investigation 20.1 of Schutz's text, where it serves as the reader's first relativistic stellar-structure calculation.

The spine of the lesson is one sentence: a star is a battle between gravity, which pulls inward, and pressure, which pushes outward — and in general relativity pressure itself adds to the gravity it is resisting, so the same battle needs a different equation than it does in Newton's theory. Each numbered section below is one beat: a short argument, a command whose printed summary illustrates it, and then the equation that the printed numbers follow from.

By varying the central pressure \(p_C\) and the equation-of-state parameters, students can construct equilibrium sequences and locate a maximum gravitational mass. The program does not evolve a star in time, so it does not simulate collapse; it computes static equilibrium configurations only.

What this program is, and what it does not model. Neutron is a teaching model of a static, non-rotating, spherically symmetric star built from one pedagogical polytropic equation of state. It has no crust-plus-core or tabulated nuclear-matter equation of state, no rotation, no magnetic field, no finite temperature, no baryonic-mass or binding-energy bookkeeping, and no time evolution: it cannot simulate collapse, a merger, or oscillation modes. Use it to see how the TOV equation differs from Newtonian hydrostatics and how a relativistic stellar-structure solver locates a surface it is not allowed to overshoot. Beats 6 and 7 collect the limits.

Beat 0 · Held up by its own pressure

Run

python main.py --gamma 1.666667 --K 5380.2 --pC 1.26e+35 --steps_per_scale 400 --max_steps 200000 \
  --output_type pressure

Look first at the global radius and mass, then at the pressure plot. The default gives a surface radius near 7.804 km and mass near 0.962591 solar masses. The table samples radius fractions 0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 0.90. The plotted pressure reaches the model surface at zero.

Task: Write down the central pressure, surface radius and total gravitational mass. Why do the five printed checkpoints not include the exact surface?

Beat 1 · Pressure gravitates too

Newtonian hydrostatic balance uses \(dp/dr=-Gm\rho/r^2\). The TOV equation used here multiplies this by three positive corrections: \((1+p/(\rho c^2))(1+4\pi r^3p/(mc^2))/(1-2Gm/(rc^2))\). For this default star the centre limit of their product is about 1.979374; at the first finite point, \(r=16.375\,\mathrm{m}\), it is about 1.97937253. The ratio changes outward: it is a centre limit, not a universal multiplier.

Run

python main.py --output_type density --pC 1.26e+35

Compare pressure and density checkpoints with Beat 0. The local \(p/(\rho c^2)\) is about 0.10911 at \(r/R\simeq0.504\), about 0.0001331 near \(r/R\simeq0.99884\), and tends to zero at the surface. The relativistic correction also depends on enclosed mass and radius, so a small pressure term alone does not establish a Newtonian interior.

Task: Identify which printed quantity you would combine with local pressure and density to assess the compactness term. Explain why the centre-limit factor should not be copied to every radial checkpoint.

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 2 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 2 · The equation of state

Run

For comparison, the default settings are --gamma 1.666667, --K 5380.2, --pC 1.26e+35.

python main.py --gamma 1.5 --K 7.374987e6 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --gamma 1.666667 --K 5.3802e3 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --gamma 2 --K 2.863463e-3 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass

The different \(K\) values keep the central density approximately fixed while (gamma) changes. Their units change with (gamma); using the same numeric \(K\) across different exponents is not a controlled comparison. Each run computes a new star.

Task: Compare the three radii and masses. Which exponent gives the most extended equilibrium here? For an input check, run python main.py --gamma 1: the solver must reject it before integration, because its surface transformation requires \(\gamma>1\).

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 6 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 3 · Starting from the center

At \(r=0\), the TOV formula contains terms with \(r\) in a denominator. The program starts at a small positive radius using the regular centre expansion: \(m\simeq4\pi\rho_C r^3/3\) and \(p\simeq p_C-Ar^2\), where \(A=2\pi G(\rho_C+p_C/c^2)(\rho_C/3+p_C/c^2)\). These set the initial pressure and mass; they are local approximations, not a second equation of state.

Run

python main.py --output_type pressure --steps_per_scale 400

The printed zero-radius row reports central quantities, while the numerical integration starts at \(r_0>0\).

Task: Why must the enclosed mass start proportional to \(r^3\), while the leading pressure correction is proportional to \(r^2\)? Locate the starting radius in driver_neutron.py after predicting what increasing --steps_per_scale will do to it.

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 2 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 4 · Resolve the march outward

Run

For comparison, the default settings are --steps_per_scale 400.

python main.py --steps_per_scale 200 --output_type mass
python main.py --steps_per_scale 400 --output_type mass
python main.py --steps_per_scale 800 --output_type mass
python main.py --steps_per_scale 1600 --output_type mass

The program takes fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) steps through the stellar interior, with nominal spacing set by a scale height divided by steps_per_scale. Doubling this parameter makes the radial grid finer and usually increases the number of retained samples. Printed radii are rounded, so identical displayed radii do not prove identical numerical answers. The observed end-to-end surface radius in this sweep should not be called an RK4 fourth-order measurement.

Task: Record sample counts and printed radii. Where does increasing resolution change a displayed digit? Compare the mass profiles. For unrounded values, open python in the program folder and run these two lines (press Enter after each):
from driver_neutron import compute_neutron_star as star
[(n, star(1.666667, 1.26e35, 5.3802e3, steps_per_scale=n)["surface_radius_m"]) for n in (200, 400, 800, 1600)]

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 2 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 5 · Stop at the surface

The surface is where \(p=0\). A full step can cross to negative pressure, making \(p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\) invalid. The solver uses \(q=p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\), which approaches zero with an approximately finite slope, to locate the surface from the last valid state. The tangent-line distance is \(-q/(dq/dr)\); its radius error is second order in the final step size in this calculation.

Run

python main.py --steps_per_scale 800 --output_type pressure

The printed radius is an estimated zero-pressure surface, beyond the last positive-pressure integration sample. The tests compare it against a separately implemented fine-grid integrator; that is an outside numerical check of this solver, not an exact physical measurement of a star.

Task: Open python in the program folder and run:
from driver_neutron import compute_neutron_star
model = compute_neutron_star(1.666667, 1.26e35, 5.3802e3, steps_per_scale=800)
model["pressure"][-2:], model["radius"][-2], model["surface_radius_m"]
The penultimate pressure is the last positive one; compare its radius with the zero-pressure endpoint. Why would accepting a negative-pressure RK4 stage be a poor way to finish?

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 2 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 6 · Read the physical checks

Every completed model is checked against two independent limits: a mathematical bound on how compact any static fluid star can be, and a physical requirement that nothing travel faster than light inside the model equation of state. They do not have to fail together. Push the central pressure up and watch each one separately.

Run

For comparison, the default settings are --pC 1.26e+35.

python main.py --pC 1e34 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e36 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e37 --output_type mass

Read both the compactness \(2GM/(Rc^2)\) and central sound speed divided by (c) in each summary. Compactness is approximately 0.203957, 0.349845, 0.462172, 0.466203. Central sound-speed ratios are approximately 0.357557, 0.566689, 0.898142, 1.423460. The last violates the model's causality check, even though its compactness remains below the Buchdahl bound \(8/9\). Passing one check does not imply passing the other.

Task: Find the first pressure in this list at which the central sound speed exceeds (c). Does the displayed model also violate the compactness bound?

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 5 and Experiment 7 in Suggested Experiments.

Beat 7 · Follow the mass turnover

Run

For comparison, the default settings are --pC 1.26e+35.

python main.py --pC 1e34 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e36 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e28 --output_type mass
python main.py --pC 1e27 --output_type mass

The masses at \(10^{34},10^{35},10^{36}\) Pa are about 0.818445, 0.963016, 0.849747 solar masses. Thus the equilibrium sequence has a maximum somewhere between \(10^{35}\) and \(10^{36}\) Pa. This calculation does not evolve a perturbation or establish dynamic stability. At \(10^{28}\) and \(10^{27}\) Pa it reports about 0.020979 and 0.010529 solar masses; extrapolating one high-density polytrope this far omits real crust and composition physics.

Task: Sketch mass versus central pressure from the five runs, marking the turnover bracket. Try python main.py --max_steps 100 and explain why it exits with an error instead of reporting a truncated star.

Experiments that go with this beat: Experiment 4 and Experiment 8 in Suggested Experiments.

Parameters

Run python main.py --help for the current command summary and python main.py --version for the model version and Build ID. Any options omitted from the command use the defaults below. The program requires Python 3.10 or later, NumPy, and Matplotlib.

ParameterDefaultUnitsDescription
--gamma1.666667dimensionlessPolytropic exponent in \(p=K\rho^\gamma\). Must be greater than 1 (Beat 2).
--pC1.26e35PaCentral pressure. Positive and finite.
--K5.3802e3depends on \(\gamma\)Polytropic constant. Its dimensions depend on \(\gamma\); see the comparison note in Beat 2.
--steps_per_scale400—Nominal radial resolution, \(N_{\rm scale}\) in Eq. 6. Integer from 50 through 10000000.
--max_steps200000—Safety limit on radial integration steps. Integer from 100 through 10000000. Reaching it before the surface raises an error (Beat 7).
--output_typepressureselectorpressure: radial pressure; density: radial density; mass: enclosed gravitational mass. Capitalization and surrounding spaces are ignored.
--log_yoffflagLogarithmic y-axis for pressure or density; --no-log_y selects linear scaling explicitly. Mass always plots on a linear axis and prints a warning if --log_y is requested with --output_type mass.

G=6.67430×10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² and c=299 792 458 m/s are fixed physical constants, not command-line flags. You can combine the listed options in one command. Close a plot before the next run.

Suggested Experiments

Suggested order: the experiments below are arranged roughly from introductory parameter exploration to more demanding sequence-building and cross-program comparisons. Several require more than one run; record the printed results of each command before comparing them.
Experiment 1 · Introductory
Profile shapes: pressure, density, and mass

Goes with Beat 0.

Run the default model with --output_type set in turn to pressure, density, and mass. Describe how each profile changes with radius, and explain in words why enclosed mass must increase outward while pressure and density decrease.

Check: all three runs share the same surface radius, 7.804 km, and total mass, 0.962591 Msun (Beat 0); only the plotted curve changes.

Experiment 2 · Introductory–Intermediate
Numerical convergence

Goes with Beat 4 and Beat 5.

Repeat the default model with --steps_per_scale = 200, 400, 800, and 1600. Compare surface radius, total mass, and compactness. Which reported quantity is most sensitive to radial refinement, and which has essentially already converged at the default resolution?

Check: surface radius is 7.803970, 7.803563, 7.803543 and 7.803139 km, and total mass is 0.96259066, 0.96259065, 0.96259064 and 0.96259064 Msun, at the four resolutions (Beat 4). Mass converges far faster than radius.

Experiment 3 · Intermediate
Comparison with Newtonian hydrostatics

Goes with Beat 1.

Run Star separately and match its central density and pressure into Neutron. Star obtains its central density from temperature and composition inputs and derives its own polytropic constant, so the two programs do not take an identical set of equation-of-state parameters; after running Star, use its central density \(\rho_C\) to set \(K=p_C/\rho_C^\gamma\) in Neutron. Compare mass, radius, and pressure and density profiles between the two programs. Relativistic differences should grow as compactness increases.

Check: the weak-field agreement demonstrated numerically in Beat 1 is the limiting case in which the two programs must agree closely; the default Neutron model, at compactness 0.3643, is far from that limit.

Experiment 4 · Intermediate
Mass–central-pressure sequence and the maximum mass

Goes with Beat 7.

Run a logarithmically spaced series of --pC values and record data["total_mass_solar"] and data["surface_radius_km"] for each. Plot mass and radius against central pressure and locate the mass maximum. The program computes equilibria only; it does not dynamically evolve the unstable branch past the turnover. Can you choose a toy polytropic equation of state whose maximum mass exceeds two solar masses? Three? Explain why such a result is not, by itself, evidence for a realistic neutron-star equation of state.

Check: the default equation of state gives 0.818445, 0.963016 and 0.849747 Msun at \(p_C=10^{34}\), \(10^{35}\) and \(10^{36}\) Pa, so the maximum lies between the last two (Beat 7).

Experiment 5 · Intermediate
Buchdahl compactness along a sequence

Goes with Beat 6.

Plot data["compactness"] for the same sequence of models used in Experiment 4. Every configuration must remain at or below 8/9 (Eq. 8). Investigate how close the chosen sequence comes to the bound, and separately track data["causality_satisfied"] across the same sequence: does the model become acausal before or after it approaches the Buchdahl bound?

Check: for the default equation of state, compactness reaches only 0.4662 by \(p_C=10^{37}\) Pa, well short of 8/9, while causality has already failed at that same central pressure (Beat 6). For this polytrope, causality is the binding limit, not Buchdahl's bound.

Experiment 6 · Intermediate–Advanced
Controlled equation-of-state stiffness comparison

Goes with Beat 2.

Because \(K\)'s dimensions depend on \(\gamma\) (Beat 2), comparing exponents at a fixed numerical \(K\) is not a controlled comparison. Instead choose a common central pressure and a target central density, and for each \(\gamma>1\) compute \(K=p_C/\rho_C^\gamma\) before running: 

python main.py --gamma 1.5 --K 7.374987e6 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --gamma 1.666667 --K 5.3802e3 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass
python main.py --gamma 2 --K 2.863463e-3 --pC 1.26e35 --output_type mass

The three K values above already target a common \(\rho_C\approx6.63345\times10^{18}\) kg/m³ at the shared \(p_C=1.26\times10^{35}\) Pa. Compare the resulting mass and radius, and describe how a stiffer equation of state (larger \(\gamma\)) changes the star at fixed central density.

Check: the middle command, \(\gamma=1.666667\), reproduces the default model of Beat 0 exactly: surface radius 7.804 km, total mass 0.962591 Msun.

Experiment 7 · Advanced
Limits of a single polytrope at high density

Goes with Beat 6.

Explore a wide central-pressure range with python main.py --pC 1e35 --output_type density --log_y and python main.py --pC 1e37 --output_type density --log_y. For each model, evaluate \(v_s^2/c^2=\gamma p/(\rho c^2)\), especially at the center, and note where the printed causality check fails. Compare the form of this equation of state with a modern piecewise polytrope or tabulated nuclear equation of state, and explain why a realistic self-bound quark-matter model generally needs an offset energy density that cannot be represented merely by choosing \(\gamma=1\) here (which this solver refuses outright; Beat 2).

Check: central sound speed rises from \(v_s/c=0.566689\) at \(p_C=10^{35}\) Pa to \(v_s/c=1.423460\) at \(p_C=10^{37}\) Pa, crossing 1 somewhere between the two (Beat 6).

Experiment 8 · Advanced · Schutz suggestion
The minimum-mass puzzle of Investigation 20.1

Goes with Beat 7.

Reduce --pC below 1e28 until the computed mass falls below 0.02 solar masses, the approximate lower bound Investigation 20.1 discusses for a physically credible neutron star. Does the numerical result invalidate that discussion? Examine how this single high-density polytrope extrapolates into the low-density regime, and identify the crust, neutron-drip, composition-change, or other low-density physics the simple equation of state leaves out entirely. Distinguish a mathematical equilibrium of the toy equation of state from a physically credible low-mass neutron star.

Check: --pC 1e28 prints 0.020979 Msun, just above the threshold, and --pC 1e27 prints 0.010529 Msun, below it (Beat 7).

Model limits

This is a static, spherical, non-rotating single-polytrope teaching model. It has no crust transition, rotation, magnetic field, finite temperature or time evolution. Its printed results test the model's equations and numerical implementation; they do not measure the masses of real neutron stars. Read the Neutron-SampleOutputs_Guide.html for the four representative figures associated with Beats 0, 4, 6 and 7.
