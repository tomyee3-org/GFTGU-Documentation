Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.1.0  |  Build 10144b08a600  |  Generated 10 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide presents three outputs generated from the supplied current source. They demonstrate square-root displacement scaling for two step distributions, now drawn with equal log-decade lengths on both axes, and show several isotropic walks escaping a circular schematic star. No external image files are required.

Reproducibility. Random2 normally produces a new realization on every run. Fixed seeds are used only for the illustrations below. With the same supplied code and Python random-number generator, the shown realizations can be regenerated exactly; changing or omitting the seeds produces different paths and statistical scatter.

Figure 1 — Default uniform-component scaling

random.seed(20260905)  # guide reproducibility only
lengths, avg_dist = run_scaled_distance_experiment(
    max_steps=4096, n_trials=100,
    step_distribution="uniform")
plot_scaled_distance(lengths, avg_dist)

For each step count, 100 independent three-dimensional walks are averaged. Every walk’s net displacement is divided by its own mean step length before averaging.

What it shows

  • The data approach a straight line on logarithmic axes that use equal decade lengths, so a slope of 1/2 is a rise of one vertical decade over two horizontal decades. A least-squares fit to all 12 plotted points gives a slope of 0.503, very close to the theoretical square-root exponent 0.5.
  • The ratios of average scaled displacement to √N range from 0.906 to 1.026; their scatter is expected with only 100 trials at each step count.
  • The default independent uniform components fill a cube and do not give isotropic directions. That anisotropy does not alter the square-root exponent being tested.

Step counts: 2 through 4,096  |  Trials per point: 100  |  Fitted log-log slope: 0.503086  |  Seed: 20260905

Figure 2 — Gaussian distribution comparison

random.seed(20260906)  # guide reproducibility only
lengths, avg_dist = run_scaled_distance_experiment(
    max_steps=4096, n_trials=100,
    step_distribution="gaussian")
plot_scaled_distance(lengths, avg_dist)

Replacing the uniform components with independent standard normal components changes the step-length distribution but not the characteristic scaling law.

What it shows

  • Equal decade lengths are used again, so the same 1/2 slope can be read by eye. The fitted slope is 0.500, again essentially one half.
  • The ratios of average scaled displacement to √N range from 0.930 to 1.041, comparable to the scatter in Figure 1.
  • This experiment illustrates the broader result: independent steps with finite variance exhibit characteristic displacement proportional to √N, rather than depending on one special random-number distribution.

Distribution: Gaussian components  |  Trials per point: 100  |  Fitted log-log slope: 0.500149  |  Seed: 20260906

Figure 3 — Isotropic walks in a schematic star

random.seed(20260019)  # guide reproducibility only
result = run_walk2d(
    reference_steps=2000, n_walks=4,
    mean_free_path=1.0, radius_factor=2.0,
    radius=None, ray_length_factor=0.6,
    step_cap=200_000)
plot_walk2d(result, corner="upper right")

Every displayed step has unit length and an angle sampled uniformly from 0 to 2π. Each path is clipped to its exact boundary crossing; the short straight continuation is a visual cue that scattering has stopped in the toy model.

What it shows

  • The default radius is 2√2000 = 89.443 distance units, twice the root-mean-square displacement expected after the reference number of fixed-length steps.
  • All four seeded walks escape. Their step counts are 4,382, 5,131, 6,401, 8,828, with a median of 5,766.
  • Individual paths differ greatly despite identical rules. The picture is a two-dimensional geometric illustration of diffusion, not a quantitative solar model or a simulation of the Sun’s convective envelope.

Radius: 89.443  |  Mean free path: 1  |  Escaped: 4 of 4  |  Median escape steps: 5,766  |  Seed: 20260019

Notes for evaluation

  • Two distinct constructions: scaled_distance uses three-dimensional component-generated steps; walk2d uses fixed-length planar steps with uniformly random direction. The latter is genuinely isotropic in the plane.
  • Boundary handling: when a display step crosses the circle, the driver computes the exact line-circle intersection rather than plotting the overshooting endpoint.
  • Interpretive limit: the star display motivates radiative diffusion, but it does not include a radial opacity profile, changing mean free path, three-dimensional first-passage statistics, or the transition from radiative to convective transport.
  • Safety cap: a path that has not escaped after step_cap steps is returned as incomplete and marked with an x rather than allowing an unbounded run.
  • Equal log scales: the two scaling figures lock one decade to the same plotted length on both axes, independent of panel shape.
  • Verification: the regression suite, including the scaled-plot aspect assertion, passed from the canonical tests directory before these figures were generated.
