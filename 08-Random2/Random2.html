Program Description

Random2 explores the square-root scaling of random walks and provides a visual, two-dimensional illustration motivated by radiative diffusion in a stellar interior. Random displacements accumulate very differently from directed motion: after \(N\) independent steps, a characteristic net displacement grows in proportion to \(\sqrt{N}\), not \(N\). This square-root behavior is a general feature of many random-walk and diffusion processes, not only radiative transport in stars.

In the Sun, energy produced by nuclear reactions in the core is transported outward through an opaque plasma. In regions where radiative diffusion dominates, repeated absorption, emission, and scattering make energy propagation resemble a random walk. Detailed diffusion-time estimates depend on the solar model and on the rapidly changing opacity and photon mean free path; the important lesson here is the random-walk scaling rather than one particular travel time.

Two Modes of Operation

scaled_distance retains the three-dimensional component-based random-walk experiment used to demonstrate the statistical law. Each step has independently generated \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) components. The default components are uniform on \([-1,1]\); an optional Gaussian distribution lets you test Schutz's observation that the \(\sqrt{N}\) behavior does not depend on this particular choice of step distribution. Each walk's net displacement is divided by its own mean step length before trial averaging.

walk2d is a visualization rather than a second version of the same step generator. Here every step has fixed length \(\lambda\) and an angle chosen uniformly from \(0\) to \(2\pi\). The result is genuinely isotropic in the plane: no direction is preferred. Several walks start at the center of a circular schematic star and continue until they cross its boundary or reach a safety limit.

Solar-model limitation: Random2 is a schematic model of radiative diffusion, not a numerical model of the complete Sun. In the real Sun, radiative transport dominates through the core and radiative zone, while convection becomes the principal energy-transport mechanism in the outer convection zone, whose base is about 200,000 km below the visible surface (roughly \(0.7R_\odot\)). The circle in the visualization should therefore be regarded as a pedagogical boundary, not as a literal simulation of one photon random-walking through every solar layer. See NASA: The Solar Interior.
Why is the star display 2D? The square-root exponent for random-walk displacement does not depend on whether the walk is in two or three dimensions, so a planar display preserves the qualitative scaling while making individual paths easy to see. The scaled_distance calculation itself is three-dimensional. Quantitative radial and first-passage statistics do depend on dimensionality, so the displayed escape-time distribution should not be interpreted as a three-dimensional solar prediction.

Physics & Equations

Component-Based Step for the Scaled-Distance Experiment

With the default step_distribution = "uniform", the three components of step \(i\) are independent:

(1)\[ \Delta x_i=2U_{x,i}-1,\qquad \Delta y_i=2U_{y,i}-1,\qquad \Delta z_i=2U_{z,i}-1, \qquad U_{x,i},U_{y,i},U_{z,i}\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1). \]

These step vectors are uniform within a cube. The resulting directions are not isotropic; directions toward the cube's body diagonals are represented more strongly than directions toward its coordinate axes. That anisotropy does not change the \(\sqrt{N}\) scaling being studied in this statistical mode.

Individual Step Length

(2)\[\ell_i=\sqrt{\Delta x_i^2+\Delta y_i^2+\Delta z_i^2}.\]

For the uniform-component model, the mean step length is the average distance from the center of the cube to a uniformly chosen point within it:

(3)\[\langle\ell\rangle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\,dx\,dy\,dz\approx0.9606.\]

Accumulated Position and Scaled Distance

(4)\[X=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta x_i,\qquad Y=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta y_i,\qquad Z=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta z_i,\qquad r_N=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2+Z^2}.\]
(5)\[\bar{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_i,\qquad d=\frac{r_N}{\bar{\ell}}.\]

Diffusion Scaling Law

For independent steps with finite variance, characteristic random-walk displacement grows as the square root of the number of steps:

(6)\[\langle d\rangle\propto\sqrt{N},\qquad \log\langle d\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\log N+\mathrm{constant}.\]

A finite Monte Carlo run will not normally produce a fitted slope of exactly \(1/2\). Increasing nTrials reduces the statistical scatter, and the measured trend should approach the theoretical value.

Fixed-Length Isotropic Step for the Star Display

In walk2d mode the direction angle is uniform and every step has length \(\lambda=\)mean_free_path:

(7)\[\theta_i\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,2\pi),\qquad \Delta x_i=\lambda\cos\theta_i,\qquad \Delta y_i=\lambda\sin\theta_i.\]

This construction is isotropic in the plane. Here \(D\) denotes the magnitude of the net displacement, not the diffusion coefficient. For \(N\) such independent steps, \(\langle D^2\rangle=N\lambda^2\), so the root-mean-square displacement is

(8)\[D_{\mathrm{rms}}=\lambda\sqrt{N}.\]

Algorithm Overview

Scaled-Distance Mode

Starting at maxSteps, the program performs nTrials independent walks, records their average scaled distance, then integer-halves the number of steps and repeats until a one-step experiment would be reached. The returned arrays are ordered from the shortest to the longest walk before plotting.

2D Star Mode

Each walk begins at the origin. A fixed-length isotropic step is generated and added to the current position. If that segment crosses the circular boundary, the code solves for the exact intersection point, stores the number of steps taken, and stops the random walk there. If the walk has not escaped after step_cap steps, it is returned as an incomplete walk rather than allowing an unbounded calculation.

2D Star Visualization

The star display is a geometric visualization of diffusion. It is not intended to reproduce the Sun's radial opacity profile, changing photon mean free path, three-dimensional geometry, or transition to convective transport.

Default Radius

Because the isotropic display step has fixed length \(\lambda\), Eq. 8 provides a natural characteristic diffusion distance. The default display radius is chosen as a multiple \(f\) of that distance for a reference step count:

(9)\[R=f\,\lambda\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad f=\texttt{radius\_factor}.\]

This formula is dimensionally consistent: \(N_{\mathrm{ref}}\) and \(f\) are dimensionless, while both \(R\) and \(\lambda\) have units of length. reference_steps therefore sets the diffusion scale used to size the drawing; it is not the maximum number of steps allowed for each displayed walk.

Exact Boundary Crossing

When a step goes from an inside point \(\mathbf p_0\) to an outside point \(\mathbf p_1\), the final plotted random-walk segment is clipped to the exact point at which it intersects the circle. With \(\mathbf p(t)=\mathbf p_0+t(\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_0)\), the crossing satisfies

(10)\[at^2+bt+c=0,\]\[a=|\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_0|^2,\qquad b=2\mathbf p_0\cdot(\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_0),\qquad c=|\mathbf p_0|^2-R^2.\]

The first root \(t\in(0,1]\) is used. A short straight segment can then be drawn beyond the boundary in the direction of the final step. This ray is only a visual cue that scattering has stopped in the toy model; it is not a physical treatment of the Sun's outer layers.

Escape Step Count

Every returned WalkPath includes steps_taken. This makes the first-passage behavior directly measurable without requiring the user to infer it from the number of plotted points. Diffusion predicts the characteristic relation

(11)\[N_{\mathrm{escape}}\propto\left(\frac{R}{\lambda}\right)^2.\]

Module Structure

Random2 follows the GFtGU physics/driver/plot/main organization.

random2_physics.py

Defines the component-based step used for the scaled-distance experiment, the fixed-length isotropic step used by the star display, the default-radius relation, and the exact line-circle crossing geometry.

generate_component_step(distribution) generate_isotropic_step(mean_free_path) default_radius(reference_steps, mean_free_path, radius_factor) circle_crossing_fraction(p0, p1, radius)

random2_driver.py

Runs both experiments, validates user parameters, records the exact boundary crossing, and reports steps_taken for every displayed walk.

run_scaled_distance_experiment( max_steps, n_trials, step_distribution) run_walk2d( reference_steps, n_walks, ...)

random2_plot.py

Produces the log-log scaling graph or the circular star visualization and reports basic escape statistics in the annotation box.

plot_scaled_distance(lengths, avg_dist) plot_walk2d(result, corner="upper right")
main.py: selects "scaled_distance" or "walk2d" and exposes all parameters intended for ordinary experimentation, including step_distribution, ray_length_factor, and step_cap.

Program Output

Scaled-distance mode produces a log-log plot of average scaled displacement versus number of steps. The points should approach a line of slope \(1/2\); finite trial counts produce visible statistical scatter.

2D star mode produces a square plot of fixed-length isotropic walks within a circular schematic star. The annotation box reports the radius, mean free path, reference step count, number of escaped walks, and—when at least one walk escapes—the median escape step count. Each WalkPath also stores its individual steps_taken value for quantitative experiments. If a walk reaches step_cap without escaping, its final point is marked with an x; an escaped path may have no terminal ray when ray_length_factor = 0.

Suggested Experiments

The experiments below are arranged in approximate order of difficulty. The first group explores the statistical square-root law; the later experiments use the 2D star visualization to study first-passage behavior and geometric scaling.

1 · Introductory — Verify the √N Law

Run the default scaled_distance experiment. On the log-log plot, the points should lie approximately along a straight line of slope \(1/2\). Do not expect an exact straight line from only 100 trials: the departures from it are part of the Monte Carlo experiment.

display = "scaled_distance" maxSteps = 4096 nTrials = 100

2 · Introductory — Reduce Statistical Scatter

Repeat the same calculation with nTrials = 25, 100, 500, 1000. As more independent walks are averaged, the points should fluctuate less and the overall trend should become more stable. Notice the trade-off between statistical precision and run time.

nTrials = 25 # then try 100, 500, 1000

3 · Introductory–Intermediate — Measure the Log-Log Slope

Go beyond judging the graph by eye. Use two well-separated plotted points, or fit a straight line to all the points in \(\log N\) versus \(\log\langle d\rangle\), and estimate the exponent \(\alpha\) in \(\langle d\rangle\propto N^\alpha\). Repeat for several nTrials values and see whether the estimate approaches \(1/2\) as the Monte Carlo noise is reduced.

expected exponent: alpha ≈ 0.5

4 · Intermediate — Change the Step Distribution

Repeat the scaled-distance calculation with Gaussian rather than uniform step components. The detailed distribution of step lengths changes, but the characteristic \(N^{1/2}\) dependence remains. This directly implements one of Schutz's suggested modifications and demonstrates that the square-root law is not peculiar to a uniform random-number distribution.

step_distribution = "uniform" # compare with "gaussian"

5 · Intermediate Programming Extension — Change the Definition of Walk Length

Test whether the square-root law depends on the program's chosen definition of scaled distance. Make a separate experimental copy of the scaled-distance calculation and omit the division by each walk's mean step length, so that the quantity averaged is simply the net displacement \(r_N\). Repeat the log-log experiment. The vertical normalization will change, but the exponent should remain close to \(1/2\). This distinguishes a universal scaling law from an arbitrary choice of units.

advanced edit: average net_distance instead of net_distance / mean_step

6 · Intermediate — Mean Free Path and Geometric Scaling

Switch to walk2d. With radius = None, multiply mean_free_path by a constant. Because the default radius is proportional to the same mean free path, the displayed geometry should look statistically similar even though all distances have been rescaled. This is a useful check that changing units does not change the underlying dimensionless random-walk problem.

display = "walk2d" mean_free_path = 1.0 # compare with 0.5 and 2.0

7 · Intermediate — Individual Walks and Statistical Variety

Display a few walks at a time and compare their shapes and escape step counts. Then increase n_walks moderately. Individual paths can look strikingly different even though they obey the same statistical rules. Keep the number small enough that the trajectories remain visually distinguishable; the purpose is to connect the statistical law with the irregular appearance of individual random walks.

n_walks = 4 # compare with 6 and 10

8 · Intermediate–Advanced — Explore the Escape-Time Distribution

Run many isotropic star walks and collect walk.steps_taken for the paths that escape. Compare the mean, median, minimum, and maximum, or make a histogram. First-passage times form a broad distribution: two walks in the same star can escape after very different numbers of steps. Check that step_cap is high enough that incomplete walks do not bias the comparison.

display = "walk2d" n_walks = 100 # inspect [w.steps_taken for w in result.walks if w.escaped]

9 · Advanced — Verify Escape Steps ∝ R²

Hold mean_free_path fixed and use several explicit star radii. For each radius, average steps_taken over many escaped walks. Diffusion predicts a characteristic first-passage step count proportional to \((R/\lambda)^2\). Test this by doubling \(R\): the characteristic escape count should increase by roughly a factor of four. Because the distribution is broad, use many walks and compare averages or medians rather than single trajectories. Confirm that nearly all walks escape; if they do not, raise step_cap so censoring does not bias the comparison.

radius = 30.0 # compare with 40.0, 50.0, 60.0 # keep mean_free_path fixed
