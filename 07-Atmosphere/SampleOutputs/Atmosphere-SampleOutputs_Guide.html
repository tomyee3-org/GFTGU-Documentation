Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide presents the three plots generated by the supplied current program for its default hybrid Earth atmosphere: pressure, density, and temperature versus altitude. No external image files are required.

The lower-atmosphere temperature points through approximately 86 km are based on U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 layer boundaries converted to geometric altitude. The 100–500 km portion uses rounded neutral temperatures from the documented NRLMSIS 2.0 example. Between supplied points the model interpolates temperature linearly, then combines hydrostatic equilibrium with the ideal-gas law using constant gravity and molecular weight.

Figure 1 — Pressure versus altitude

# h_points and T_points are the supplied arrays in main.py
params = AtmosphereParameters(
    planet_name="Earth", g_accel=9.81, mu=28.97,
    p0=1.013e5, h_points=h_points, T_points=T_points,
    output_type="Pressure")
result = AtmosphereModel(params).run()
plot_atmosphere(extract_output(result))

The model begins at 101.3 kPa and integrates upward with an explicit Euler hydrostatic step. On a linear vertical scale, the many-orders-of-magnitude decrease compresses most upper-atmosphere pressures close to the horizontal axis.

What it shows

  • Near 10.01 km, the computed pressure is 26.070 kPa; near 50.02 km it is 70.152 Pa.
  • At about 100.00 km the pressure has fallen to 0.02137 Pa, and by 499.98 km it is 1.170e-08 Pa.
  • A semilogarithmic plotting experiment is useful when the goal is to inspect the high-altitude slope rather than the full linear-scale falloff.

Surface pressure: 101.3 kPa  |  Initial altitude step: 41.892 m  |  Stored positive-pressure points: 13,653  |  Last stored altitude: 571.913 km

Figure 2 — Density versus altitude

params.output_type = "Density"
result = AtmosphereModel(params).run()
plot_atmosphere(extract_output(result))

Density is calculated from the pressure, prescribed temperature, and mean molecular weight using the ideal-gas relation. Like pressure, it spans so many orders of magnitude that its upper-atmosphere structure is visually compressed on a linear axis.

What it shows

  • The computed reference-level density is 1.2325 kg/m³.
  • Near 50.02 km, density is 9.087e-04 kg/m³; near 100.00 km it is 3.944e-07 kg/m³.
  • Temperature structure matters because density is proportional to p/T, so this curve is not simply a rescaled copy of pressure.

Density at 0 km: 1.232471 kg/m³  |  At 50 km: 9.087e-04 kg/m³  |  At 100 km: 3.944e-07 kg/m³  |  At 500 km: 3.308e-14 kg/m³

Figure 3 — Temperature versus altitude

params.output_type = "Temperature"
result = AtmosphereModel(params).run()
plot_atmosphere(extract_output(result))

Temperature exposes the supplied profile directly: cooling through the troposphere, stratospheric warming, a cold mesopause region, and strong thermospheric warming. The sharp decline above 500 km belongs to the program’s mathematical continuation, not to the NRLMSIS data.

What it shows

  • The supplied profile begins at 288.15 K, reaches about 186.947 K near 86 km, then rises to about 800.1 K near 150 km.
  • The prescribed thermospheric temperature reaches 1,240 K at 400 km and remains there through 500 km.
  • Above the highest supplied point, the closure law T = beta p0.5 forces temperature toward zero as the integration approaches its numerical zero-pressure boundary. That portion should not be read as a realistic far-upper-atmosphere prediction.

T(0): 288.15 K  |  T(86 km): 186.947 K  |  T(150 km): 800.1 K  |  T(500 km): 1240.0 K

Notes for evaluation

  • Model scope: gravity and mean molecular weight are held constant with altitude. The thermospheric temperatures are illustrative inputs, but the resulting high-altitude pressure and density are not NRLMSIS-quality predictions.
  • Upper profile provenance: the supplied 100–500 km values come from the documented NRLMSIS 2.0 CCMC Instant Run for 15 October 2024 at 12:00 UTC, 55° N, 45° E, using observational solar and geomagnetic inputs for that date. They are not universal standard temperatures.
  • Numerical method: the driver uses 200 Euler steps per base scale height and permits up to 50,000 points; if necessary, it restarts with a doubled step size to reach the numerical boundary.
  • Boundary meaning: the zero-pressure endpoint is a stopping condition for this simplified calculation, not a sharply defined physical top of a real atmosphere.
  • Verification: all 72 supplied tests passed from the canonical tests directory before these figures were generated.
