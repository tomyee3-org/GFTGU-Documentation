Atmosphere

Representative Sample Outputs

Current program version: 1.6.0   Build: cab9929fa0fb
Guide prepared: 2026-09-26

Purpose. These eight figures, one for each Beat of the Help file, give a compact visual survey of what Atmosphere computes. The program integrates hydrostatic equilibrium upward from a surface pressure, using the ideal-gas law and a temperature profile that the user supplies; it does not compute the temperature. Each run shows one plot and prints a checkpoint table. Where a Beat uses several commands, the figure shows one of them and the headline values also cover the others.

Beat 0 — Pressure versus altitude for the default Earth

python main.py

Default Earth run. Pressure against altitude from the surface to the numerical top.

What it shows

  • Pressure falls fastest near the ground, where the air is densest.
  • Most of the column mass already sits below 11 km.
  • The curve continues smoothly through the supplied layers and ends where the integrator reaches zero pressure.
  • Gravity is held constant; the slope is minus g times density.

Headline values

  • Surface: 1.013e5 Pa, 1.2249 kg m−3, 288.15 K.
  • 11 019 m: 22 499 Pa, 22.21% of the surface pressure, so about 78% of the column sits below that height.
  • Column mass p0/g = 1.033e4 kg m−2.
  • 86 km: 0.30439 Pa. Last stored altitude 572.4 km.

Beat 1 — Density versus altitude for the default Earth

python main.py --output_type density

The same integration as Beat 0, plotted as mass density.

What it shows

  • Density follows p μ mu / (kB T). The printed table is identical to Beat 0.
  • Dividing density by the p/T column recovers the same constant at every row.
  • Density need not fall as fast as pressure when temperature is falling.
  • The plotted quantity is the only thing --output_type changes.

Headline values

  • Surface density 1.2249 kg m−3, within 0.01% of the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 sea-level value 1.2250.
  • p/T at the surface is 351.55 Pa K−1; density / (p/T) = 3.484×10−3 kg K J−1 = μ mu/kB.
  • At 86 km, density is 5.6731×10−6 kg m−3.

Beat 2 — An isothermal atmosphere and its scale height

python main.py --h_points 0,8000,16000,24000,32000 --T_points 288.15,288.15,288.15,288.15,288.15

An isothermal 288.15 K Earth atmosphere sampled every 8 km.

What it shows

  • Equal altitude steps multiply the pressure by the same factor.
  • The exact isothermal model multiplies pressure by exp(−Δh/H) each 8 km. The printed ratio is the Euler value 0.3862; exp(−8000/8430.15) is 0.3871, 0.24% larger. H = p0/(g ρ0) = kB T / (g μ mu).
  • The two readings of H, from the table ratios and from the surface row, are two views of the same model.

Headline values

  • Surface scale height H = 8430 m.
  • Each 8 km step multiplies pressure by 0.3862.
  • The first integration step is H/200 = 42.15 m for this warm isothermal profile; on the default Earth run it is 27.35 m, sized from the 186.95 K mesopause.

Beat 3 — A heavier gas: mean molecular mass 44

python main.py --mu 44

Default Earth temperatures and gravity, but μ = 44 instead of 28.97.

What it shows

  • Raising μ shrinks the scale height and makes pressure fall more rapidly with altitude.
  • The other Beat 3 runs change g or p0 one dial at a time.
  • Raising p0 by ten multiplies every pressure by ten and leaves the fraction p(11 km)/p0 alone.

Headline values

  • Default p(11 019 m)/p0 = 0.2221.
  • With --mu 44 the same fraction is 0.1017 (10 304 Pa over 1.013e5 Pa).
  • H scales as T/(μ g): 8.43 km at the default surface, 5.55 km at μ = 44.
  • The other Beat 3 commands: --mu 18 gives p(11 km)/p0 = 0.3927; --g_accel 19.62 gives 0.04930; --p0 1.013e6 leaves the fraction at 0.2221.

Beat 4 — The supplied temperature profile

python main.py --output_type temperature

Default Earth temperature against altitude, including the upper-atmosphere closure above 500 km.

What it shows

  • Four supplied layers: troposphere cooling, stratosphere warming, mesosphere cooling, thermosphere rising.
  • Local scale height tracks temperature.
  • Above 500 km the curve is the T = β p0.5 closure, not a measured thermosphere.

Headline values

  • Layer temperatures include 288.15 K at the surface, 216.65 K from 11 to 20 km, 186.95 K at 86 km, and 1240 K from 400 to 500 km.
  • Pressure falls by a factor 4.50 over the first 11 km, and by about 4700 from 200 km (6.7556e-05 Pa) to 500 km (1.437e-08 Pa).
  • Seven rounded NRLMSIS 2.0 temperatures are used from 100 to 500 km.
  • The other Beat 4 command, --h_points 0,50000,100000 --T_points 288,200,350, prints 83.933 Pa at 50 km and 0.14095 Pa at 100 km. The density fall factor from 50 to 100 km is the pressure fall factor times 350/200 = 1.75.

Beat 5 — Another world: Venus

python main.py --planet_name Venus --g_accel 8.87 --mu 44 --p0 9.2e6 \
  --h_points 0,10000,20000,30000,40000,50000,60000 \
  --T_points 735,658,579,495,416,348,263

Venus teaching profile. The Mars and Jupiter runs of Beat 5 are the same kind of figure.

What it shows

  • Nothing in the equations is specific to the Earth.
  • “Thick” can mean column mass p0/g or it can mean scale height; the rankings need not agree.
  • Venus has about a hundred Earth columns; Jupiter’s scale height is the largest of the four worlds because μ is so small.

Headline values

  • Venus surface: 9.2e6 Pa, 66.24 kg m−3, 735 K. Column mass p0/g = 1.037e6 kg m−2.
  • Venus 10 km: 4.6835e6 Pa (about 2% below the 4.80 MPa compilation value). 60 km: 20 022 Pa (about 16% below 23.9 kPa).
  • Earth column 1.033e4 kg m−2; Venus/Earth column ratio about 100.
  • Mars (g = 3.72 m s−2, μ = 44, p0 = 610 Pa, T0 = 210 K): H = 10.67 km, column p0/g = 164 kg m−2.
  • Jupiter slice (g = 24.8 m s−2, μ = 2.2, p0 = 1e5 Pa, T0 = 165 K): H = 25.14 km, column 4032 kg m−2.

Beat 6 — The program against an exact isothermal atmosphere

python main.py --h_points 0,100000 --T_points 288.15,288.15

An isothermal 288.15 K atmosphere from 0 to 100 km, for which the exact answer p0 e−h/H is known. The second Beat 6 run is the default Earth run of Beat 0.

What it shows

  • The forward-Euler step runs slightly low, and the gap grows with height.
  • The size of the gap follows 1 − (h/H)/(2N) with N = 200.
  • Against the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 the default run is about 18% low at 86 km, mostly because gravity is held constant.

Headline values

  • Exact isothermal pressure at 100 km: 0.71438 Pa. Printed value 0.69344 Pa, 2.93% low.
  • Estimate 1 − (h/H)/400 = 1 − 11.86/400 = 0.970, matching the measured 2.93%.
  • Default run at 86 km: 0.30439 Pa against the standard-atmosphere 0.3734 Pa (ratio 0.815).

Beat 7 — Where the curve stops

python main.py --h_points 0,20000 --T_points 288.15,216.65 --output_type temperature

A profile supplied only to 20 km. The curve follows the straight line down to 216.65 K at 20 km, then falls toward zero where the calculation stops.

What it shows

  • Above the last supplied altitude the temperature is the closure T = β p0.5, an assumption, not a result.
  • The endpoint is where the numerical pressure reaches zero. It is a stopping rule, not a sharp physical top of the atmosphere.
  • A real atmosphere at 30 km is not near zero kelvin: the standard atmosphere’s 228.65 K at 32 162 m appears in the default table.

Headline values

  • Last supplied point 20 km at 216.65 K; the curve ends at 32.58 km, about 13 km above it, at 0.58 K.
  • Default run: the temperature stays at 1240 K to 500 km and the curve ends at 572.4 km at 0.66 K.
  • Above its last supplied point the default run’s closure adds 72.4 km; the 20 km run’s adds 12.6 km.

Notes for evaluation

  • The model holds gravity and the mean molecular mass constant with height, takes the temperature profile as input, and integrates with a first-order Euler step that lands on every supplied temperature breakpoint and shortens a step that would change temperature by more than about 5% of the temperature at the start of that step. A proposed step shorter than about 10−5 of the local scale height is left alone. Its pressures are illustrations of the equations, not predictions.
  • The default Earth profile combines U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 layer values through about 86 km, converted to geometric altitude, with seven rounded NRLMSIS 2.0 temperatures from 100 to 500 km for one date and place. The Venus profile is from a secondary compilation; the Mars and Jupiter profiles are round-number illustrations.
  • Above the highest supplied altitude the temperature follows a mathematical closure that ends the calculation at a finite altitude. That endpoint is a stopping rule.
  • Headline values come from the printed checkpoint table or from the printed Numerical top line of the same run.
