SphereGravity

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.4.0  |  Build 217c2c26a44a  |  Updated 25 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide is for an instructor who has the SphereGravity Help (SphereGravity-claude.html) open. It shows one figure for each Beat of the Help that has a figure of its own, made with a command from that Beat, together with the values the run prints. Beats 2 and 3 change only the printed table, so their plot is the Beat 6 figure; they are covered under Notes for evaluation. It is not a retelling of the tutorial. No external image files are required.

SphereGravity tests Newton's shell theorem numerically. It cuts a thin spherical shell of radius 1 into tiles, adds the inverse-square pulls of the tiles at radii from 0 to 4.995 (G = 1), and plots either the pull or its relative difference from the theorem. Every run also prints the tiles' mass beside the exact mass and a comparison table at chosen radii, with a leading-order estimate of the error. The program has no random numbers, so every command below reproduces the figure and numbers shown.

Beat 0 — Nothing inside, a point outside

python main.py --outputType acceleration

The pull of the default tiled shell (100 divisions) is zero inside and falls off as the inverse square outside, except in a narrow band on either side of the shell.

What it shows

  • Inside the shell the curve lies on zero; outside it falls to a quarter each time the distance doubles.
  • Just inside the shell the curve dips slightly below zero, and just outside it overshoots before falling: the tiles are too coarse there.
  • The curve has a gap at r = 1, the omitted surface point.

Headline values

  • Shell mass: numerical midpoint 0.012567, continuum 0.012566, relative difference 4.1125e-05.
  • Predicted g at r = 2: 0.0031419 (expected 0.0031416); at r = 4: 0.00078544 (expected 0.0007854).
  • Predicted g at r = 0.5: -9.1945e-07 (expected 0).
  • Plotted extremes beside the shell: -0.000855 at r = 0.985 and 0.013031 at r = 1.015.

Beat 1 — The shell as tiles

python main.py --nDiv 10

With ten divisions each way the tiling error is large enough to see everywhere near the shell.

What it shows

  • The tiles carry more mass than the smooth shell, by 0.41%.
  • Beside the shell the error reaches almost half the pull, with opposite signs inside and outside.
  • Away from the shell the plotted error falls towards zero, because the plot compares with the tiles' own mass.

Headline values

  • Shell mass: numerical midpoint 0.012618, continuum 0.012566, relative difference 0.0041242; latitude step h = 0.31416.
  • Table at r = 2: relative difference 0.009588, h^2 estimate 0.0091385; at r = 0.5: g/M -0.0080246 (marked *).
  • Plotted extremes: 0.464 at r = 0.995 and -0.463 at r = 1.005; side peaks -0.076 at r = 0.845 and 0.077 at r = 1.17.
  • Default run (Beat 0): mass relative difference 4.1125e-05, a hundred times smaller.

Beat 4 — Ten times the divisions, a hundredth of the error

python main.py --nDiv 1000

With a thousand divisions the error is visible only in two thin spikes of about 1% at the shell.

What it shows

  • Away from the shell, every error falls by a factor of about a hundred for each tenfold refinement, at the same radius.
  • The printed errors agree with the h^2 estimate at every unmarked radius.
  • Only the rows at 0.995 and 1.005 are still marked with *.

Headline values

  • Mass relative difference 4.1123e-07; with --nDiv 10000, 4.1123e-09.
  • Relative difference at r = 2: 9.1386e-07 (estimate 9.1385e-07); with --nDiv 10000, 9.1385e-09.
  • g/M at r = 0.5: -7.3109e-07; with --nDiv 10000, -7.3108e-09.
  • Plotted spikes: -0.0094 at r = 0.995 and 0.0095 at r = 1.005.

Beat 5 — The surface is hard

python main.py --nDiv 10000 --radii 0.995 1.005 1.01 1.05 1.1

With ten thousand divisions even the points 0.005 from the shell are resolved, and the spikes shrink to about 8e-05.

What it shows

  • No row is marked: the step h is now small compared with the distance 0.005 to the shell.
  • The printed error agrees with the h^2 estimate at every radius, including those next to the shell.
  • The difference of the pulls just outside and just inside the shell is the jump 4πε of the theorem, less the fall-off from r = 1 to 1.005.

Headline values

  • r = 0.995: g/M -8.2331e-05 (estimate -8.2246e-05); r = 1.005: relative difference 8.3158e-05 (estimate 8.3072e-05).
  • Predicted g: 0.012443 at r = 1.005 and -1.0346e-06 at r = 0.995, a difference of 0.012444.
  • r = 1.1: relative difference 2.4936e-07.
  • Same radii at the default 100 divisions (python main.py --radii 0.995 1.005 1.01 1.05 1.1): g/M 0.15216 and relative difference -0.15117, with estimates -0.82246 and 0.83072 marked *; r = 1.1 gives 0.0025682, unmarked.

Beat 6 — The relative-difference plot

python main.py

The default plot runs from about -0.15 to +0.15, set by the two points beside the shell; everywhere else the curve lies on zero at this scale.

What it shows

  • The two spikes are the 15% errors printed at r = 0.995 and 1.005.
  • The plot divides by the tiles' own mass, so far from the shell it tends to zero, while the table, which divides by the exact mass, tends to the mass error.
  • Inside the shell the plotted and printed residuals are almost equal.

Headline values

  • Plotted extremes: 0.1521 at r = 0.995 and -0.1512 at r = 1.005.
  • Far field: table 4.6409e-05 at r = 5, of which 4.1125e-05 is the mass error; plotted 5.3e-06 at r = 4.995.
  • r = 2: table 9.1423e-05, plotted 5.03e-05.
  • r = 0.5: table g/M -7.3167e-05, plotted -7.3164e-05.

Beat 7 — Epsilon only sets the scale

python main.py --epsilon 0.002 --outputType acceleration

Doubling the mass per unit area doubles every pull; the curve has the shape of Beat 0 on twice the vertical scale.

What it shows

  • Every mass and pull is twice its Beat 0 value.
  • Every relative difference, g/M and h^2 estimate is unchanged.
  • The shell's geometry is unchanged: ε only sets how much mass the shell carries.

Headline values

  • Shell mass: numerical midpoint 0.025134, continuum 0.025133, relative difference 4.1125e-05.
  • Predicted g at r = 2: 0.0062838 (Beat 0: 0.0031419); relative difference 9.1423e-05 in both runs.
  • g/M at r = 0.5: -7.3167e-05, as in Beat 0.
  • Plotted peak 0.026061 at r = 1.015 (Beat 0: 0.013031).

Beat 8 — Coarse tilings and the hard cases

python main.py --nDiv 1

With one division the shell is a single ring at the equator, which pulls a test mass on its axis even inside, and falls short of a point mass outside.

What it shows

  • Inside, the ring pulls the test mass towards its plane, so the interior is far from empty.
  • Outside, the plotted difference from a point mass of the ring's own mass fades as the distance grows.
  • Every row of the table is marked: h = π is not small anywhere.

Headline values

  • Shell mass: numerical midpoint 0.019739, continuum 0.012566, relative difference 0.5708 (π/2 - 1).
  • Table: g/M 0.56199 at r = 0.5; relative difference 0.12397 at r = 2 and 0.48105 at r = 5.
  • Plotted: 0.385 at r = 0.705, -0.644 at r = 1.005, -0.057 at r = 4.995.
  • python main.py --radii 1 is rejected: radius must not be the shell radius 1, where the field is discontinuous; python main.py --nDiv 100001 is rejected: value must not exceed 100000.

Notes for evaluation

  • Beats 2 and 3: --radii changes only the printed table, so their plot is the Beat 6 figure. Beat 2 (--radii 2 5 10 100 1000): the relative difference falls from 9.1423e-05 at r = 2 to 4.1125e-05 at r = 1000, the mass error. Beat 3 (--radii 0 0.25 0.5 0.75 0.9): g/M is 1.2109e-16 at the centre and -2.3403e-05, -7.3167e-05, -0.00032343 and -0.0021036 outwards, each within about 3% of its estimate.
  • Two normalizations: the table divides by the exact mass 4πε, the plot by the tiles' mass; compare a plotted value with a printed one only through Eq. (17) of the Help.
  • The * mark: a marked row is one where the step h is not small compared with the distance to the shell, or with 1; its h^2 estimate does not apply.
  • Run time: the plotted profile uses the explicit textbook loops, so the 10000-division runs of Beats 4 and 5 take about ten seconds.
