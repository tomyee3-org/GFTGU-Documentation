What the program calculates

Neutron integrates the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff pressure equation and the enclosed-mass equation outward from the centre of a static, spherical relativistic star. The surface is the radius at which the pressure reaches zero.

The equation of state is the pedagogical polytrope p = K rho^gamma. It is useful for investigating relativistic hydrostatic balance, compactness, numerical convergence, and qualitative mass–radius behaviour, but it is not a modern microphysical equation of state for quantitative neutron-star predictions.

What it shows

  • The integration finds a surface radius of 7.804 km from the zero-pressure condition.
  • The regular central expansion avoids evaluating the spherical equations directly at the coordinate singularity r = 0.
  • The final surface point is obtained by extrapolating p^((gamma−1)/gamma), the variable with regular leading behaviour near a polytropic surface.

Figure 4 — Central-pressure sequence

# Documentation experiment: Mass versus central pressure
central_pressures = logspace(33, 37, 33)  # Pa
for pC in central_pressures:
    model = compute_neutron_star(gamma, pC, K)

A family of equilibrium models is generated by changing only the central pressure. The shaded region is not a solver failure: it marks where this simple polytropic equation of state predicts a central sound speed greater than light and therefore ceases to be physically admissible.

What it shows

  • Among the 33 logarithmically spaced examples, the largest sampled mass is 0.963016 Msun at pC = 1.000e+35 Pa, with radius 8.130 km.
  • The central causality limit for the selected polytrope occurs at approximately pC = 1.711e+36 Pa.
  • The mass maximum and the decreasing radius illustrate how a sequence can reveal structure that no single radial profile shows.

Notes for evaluation

  • Relativistic model: the pressure gradient includes the standard TOV corrections for pressure’s contribution to gravity, enclosed energy, and curved spatial geometry.
  • Numerical method: coupled pressure and mass equations are advanced with classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta steps; a dynamic radial grid avoids a fixed array limit, and trial steps are halved near a surface crossing when necessary.
  • Built-in safeguards: input validation, compactness monitoring, causality reporting, and the Buchdahl check make unphysical or numerically problematic choices visible.
  • Interpretive limit: a one-piece polytrope cannot represent crust transitions, composition changes, nuclear interactions, or observationally calibrated neutron-star matter. Numerical precision should not be mistaken for physical accuracy.
  • Suggested investigations: the documentation proposes profile comparison, resolution convergence, Newtonian comparison, mass–central-pressure sequences, the minimum-mass puzzle, the Buchdahl limit, controlled stiffness changes, and exploration of the single-polytrope model’s limits.
  • Build verification: the executable and documentation both identify version 1.2.0, build 685bb0e10e01. In accordance with the preparation instructions, no regression suite was run.
