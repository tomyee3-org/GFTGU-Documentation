Overview

Neutron computes static, spherically symmetric stellar configurations in general relativity. It integrates the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) pressure equation and the enclosed-mass equation outward from the center until the pressure reaches zero.

Matter is represented by one simple polytropic equation of state, \(p=K\rho^\gamma\). This is intentionally a pedagogical polytropic model, not a modern tabulated neutron-star equation of state.

By varying central pressure and the EOS parameters, students can construct equilibrium sequences and identify a maximum gravitational mass. The program does not evolve stars in time, so it does not simulate collapse; it computes static equilibrium configurations only.

Correction to the historical implementation The complete TOV pressure source is \(m+4\pi r^3p/c^2\). The original Java source omitted the factor \(p\) from this term even though its written documentation displayed the correct equation. The revised Python program uses the complete TOV expression.

Output Modes

Every run computes radius, pressure, density, and enclosed gravitational mass. output_type selects which profile is displayed.

output_typeDisplayed quantityPlot units

print_model_summary() reports surface radius, total mass, central density, compactness \(2GM/(Rc^2)\), the Buchdahl check, radial sample count, and nominal radial spacing. Pressure and density may be shown on a logarithmic scale with log_y=True.

Module Structure

main.py

Entry point and user configuration.

  • Sets EOS and central pressure.
  • Selects output and radial resolution.
  • Prints the global model summary and plots the chosen profile.

physics_neutron.py

Relativistic stellar-structure equations.

  • eos_density() — polytropic EOS inversion.
  • structure_derivatives() — complete TOV pressure and mass equations.
  • central_state() — regular small-\(r\) expansion.
  • rk4_step() — coupled fourth-order Runge–Kutta step.

driver_neutron.py

Validates inputs, integrates outward on dynamically growing arrays, locates the \(p=0\) surface, checks compactness, and returns full profiles plus model properties.

plot_neutron.py

Plots radius in km and mass in solar masses, optionally uses logarithmic pressure/density axes, and prints the model summary.

Newtonian Hydrostatics

For a spherical Newtonian star, hydrostatic equilibrium is

\[ \frac{dp}{dr}=-\frac{Gm(r)\rho(r)}{r^2}. \tag{1} \](1)

This is adequate when pressure-energy and compactness are small. Neutron stars require the relativistic corrections contained in the TOV equation.

The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) Equation

For a static, spherically symmetric perfect fluid, with \(\rho\) denoting mass density so that the energy density is \(\rho c^2\),

\[ \frac{dp}{dr} =-\frac{G\left(\rho+\dfrac{p}{c^2}\right) \left(m+\dfrac{4\pi r^3p}{c^2}\right)} {r\left(r-\dfrac{2Gm}{c^2}\right)}. \tag{2} \](2)

The two pressure-dependent factors represent relativistic pressure-energy contributions, while the denominator contains the compactness factor \(1-2Gm/(rc^2)\).

Historical implementation error The original Java source omitted the factor \(p\) in \(4\pi r^3p/c^2\). The revised Python implementation corrects that inherited error; old numerical results produced by the incomplete expression should not be used as reference values for the corrected program.

If \(r-2Gm/c^2\le0\), the code stops with an explanatory error rather than continuing through the singular TOV denominator.

Polytropic Equation of State

\[ p=K\rho^\gamma,\qquad \rho=\left(\frac{p}{K}\right)^{1/\gamma}. \tag{3} \](3)

The default \(\gamma\approx5/3\), \(K=5.3802\times10^3\) combination is useful as a simple pedagogical polytrope and is related to the ideal non-relativistic degenerate-neutron EOS. At the extreme densities reached inside neutron stars, however, realistic matter requires relativistic and strong-interaction nuclear physics. The default is therefore not a modern precision neutron-star EOS.

Comparing stiffness Because the dimensions of \(K\) depend on \(\gamma\), compare different exponents by choosing a common central reference state and computing \(K=p_C/\rho_C^\gamma\) separately for each \(\gamma\).

Mass Integral

In the standard TOV formulation using Schwarzschild areal radius,

\[ \frac{dm}{dr}=4\pi r^2\rho. \tag{4} \](4)

This is already the relativistic TOV mass equation; no extra spatial metric factor belongs on its right-hand side. The returned mass array is the enclosed gravitational mass-energy expressed in kilograms, and the plotting routine converts it to solar masses.

Buchdahl's Theorem

Under Buchdahl's assumptions — including a static, spherically symmetric, isotropic perfect fluid whose density does not increase outward —

\[ R\ge\frac{9}{4}\frac{GM}{c^2} =\frac{9}{8}r_s,\qquad r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}. \tag{5} \](5)

Equivalently, \(2GM/(Rc^2)\le8/9\). The program reports the compactness of every completed model. Buchdahl's theorem supplies a bound; a given polytropic sequence need not approach equality.

Maximum Mass and Stability

Along many one-parameter sequences of cold relativistic stellar models, the gravitational mass first rises with central pressure or density, reaches a maximum, and then turns over. Under the usual turning-point interpretation, the first maximum marks the onset of the fundamental radial instability.

Neutron computes equilibrium configurations only. It can display models on either side of a mass maximum, but it does not dynamically evolve an unstable star or simulate black-hole formation.

The maximum mass found here is a property of the assumed polytropic EOS, not a precision prediction for real neutron stars.

Radial Integration Scheme

Pressure and enclosed mass form a coupled pair of first-order ODEs. The revised solver advances both with classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) rather than the historical first-order forward-Euler march.

Python lists grow as required, so there is no fixed 2000-element storage limit and no resolution-degrading step doubling merely to fit the star inside a preallocated array.

Convergence test Repeat a model with steps_per_scale = 400, 800, and 1600. Surface radius and total mass should settle as the radial grid is refined.

Step Sizing and the Scale Height

The central density is \(\rho_C=(p_C/K)^{1/\gamma}\). Following the original exercise, define

\[ \ell=\frac{\sqrt{p_C/G}}{\rho_C}. \tag{6} \](6)

The nominal radial spacing is

\[ \Delta r=\frac{\ell}{N_{\rm scale}}, \tag{7} \](7)

where steps_per_scale supplies \(N_{\rm scale}\). The default is 400. Near the surface, the solver may temporarily shorten the final trial interval to avoid evaluating the EOS at negative pressure.

Initial Conditions at the Stellar Center

The TOV equation is formally singular at \(r=0\), although the physical solution is regular. The solver therefore starts at \(r_0=\Delta r\) using the central expansion

\[ m(r_0)\simeq\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_Cr_0^3, \]
\[ p(r_0)\simeq p_C -2\pi G\left(\rho_C+\frac{p_C}{c^2}\right) \left(\frac{\rho_C}{3}+\frac{p_C}{c^2}\right)r_0^2. \tag{8} \](8)

This preserves the regular central boundary conditions while improving on the historical approximation \(p(r_0)=p_C\).

Locating the Stellar Surface

The surface is defined by \(p=0\). The solver integrates outward on the nominal grid and shortens the final step when the local pressure slope shows that the zero lies within the next interval. Surface radius and enclosed mass are then evaluated at that zero-pressure location.

This removes both the old one-grid-step ambiguity in the reported radius and the historical need to double the radial spacing when a fixed 2000-point array was exhausted.

Suggested Experiments

1. Mass–Central-Pressure Sequence

Run a logarithmically spaced series of pC values and record data["total_mass_solar"] and data["surface_radius_km"]. Plot mass and radius against central pressure and locate any mass maximum. The program computes equilibria; it does not dynamically collapse the unstable branch.

2. Numerical Convergence

Repeat the default model with steps_per_scale = 200, 400, 800, and 1600. Compare surface radius, total mass, and compactness.

3. Comparison with Newtonian Hydrostatics

Use the Star program with the same \(p_C\), \(K\), and \(\gamma\). Identical EOS inputs imply the same central density, so compare mass, radius, pressure profile, and density profile rather than central density. Relativistic differences grow as compactness increases.

4. Buchdahl Check

Examine data["compactness"] for a sequence of models. Configurations satisfying Buchdahl's assumptions must remain at or below \(8/9\). Investigate how close the chosen sequence comes to the bound; the theorem does not require saturation.

5. Controlled EOS-Stiffness Comparison

Choose a common \(p_C\) and target \(\rho_C\). For each \(\gamma>1\), calculate \(K=p_C/\rho_C^\gamma\) and compare the resulting mass-radius models. This keeps the central reference state comparable.

6. Limits of a Single Polytrope

Explore a wide central-pressure range and note when the simple EOS produces extreme densities or compactness. Compare its assumptions with modern piecewise or tabulated nuclear equations of state. A realistic self-bound quark-matter EOS generally includes an offset energy density and cannot be represented merely by setting \(\gamma=1\) and \(K=c^2/3\) in this solver.
