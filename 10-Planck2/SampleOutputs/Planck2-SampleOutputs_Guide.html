Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.0.4  |  Build 5b6ad69fefb7  |  Generated 5 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide presents four spectra generated from the supplied current source. Together they show all three supported spectral quantities, the difference between wavelength and frequency peaks, and a cosmic-microwave-background example. No external image files are required.

Planck2 evaluates black-body spectra on a uniform grid in the dimensionless variable x = hc/(λkT) = hν/(kT), then maps the results into physical wavelength or frequency. Every plot reports the sampled peak, the dimensionless shape-function area, the numerical physical integral, and its exact infinite-domain reference.

Figure 1 — Solar-temperature wavelength radiance

domain = PlanckDomain(
    x_min=0.01, x_max=100.0,
    x_low=0.05, x_high=20.0)
result = run_planck2(
    T=5900.0, quantity="wavelength",
    n_steps=2000, domain=domain)
plot_planck2(result, y_frac_window=0.003)

The supplied defaults approximate the Sun’s continuum as a 5,900 K black body. The vertical dotted line marks the largest sampled value of spectral radiance per unit wavelength.

What it shows

  • The sampled dimensionless peak is x = 4.959505, giving a wavelength of 491.703 nm, in the visible range.
  • The physical integral is 2.187105e+07 W m−2 sr−1. Its relative difference from the exact infinite-domain value is -6.899e-07.
  • The dimensionless wavelength shape uses x5/(exp(x) − 1); its area is not itself the bolometric wavelength integral because the physical-coordinate integration includes a Jacobian.

Peak wavelength: 491.703 nm  |  Peak x: 4.959505  |  Dimensionless area: 122.081167  |  Samples: 2,001

Figure 2 — Solar-temperature frequency radiance

result = run_planck2(
    T=5900.0, quantity="frequency",
    n_steps=2000, domain=domain)
plot_planck2(result, y_frac_window=0.003)

The same black body is now expressed as spectral radiance per unit frequency. This is a different spectral density, not merely the first curve with a relabeled horizontal axis.

What it shows

  • The frequency form uses the shape x3/(exp(x) − 1), whose sampled peak is x = 2.809720 at 345.416 THz.
  • Converting that peak frequency to a wavelength gives c/νpeak = 867.917 nm—not the 491.703 nm peak of the wavelength-density curve.
  • Despite the different-looking spectrum and peak coordinate, integrating either radiance density over its own physical coordinate gives the same bolometric radiance.

Peak frequency: 345.416 THz  |  Equivalent c/ν: 867.917 nm  |  Physical integral: 2.187105e+07 W m−2 sr−1

Figure 3 — Solar-temperature frequency energy density

result = run_planck2(
    T=5900.0, quantity="energy_density",
    n_steps=2000, domain=domain)
plot_planck2(result, y_frac_window=0.003)

Frequency energy density and frequency radiance share the same dimensionless shape and peak frequency. Their magnitudes and physical-integral units differ by the isotropic-radiation factor 4π/c.

What it shows

  • The peak remains at 345.416 THz and x = 2.809720, exactly matching Figure 2’s sampled location.
  • The integrated energy density is 0.916766578 J/m³, compared with the exact value 0.916767210 J/m³.
  • The energy-density/radiance conversion factor is 4π/c = 4.191690e-08 s/m; applying it to the Figure 2 integral reproduces this result.

Peak frequency: 345.416 THz  |  Physical integral: 0.916766578 J/m³  |  Relative integral error: -6.899e-07

Figure 4 — Cosmic microwave background example

result = run_planck2(
    T=2.725, quantity="frequency",
    n_steps=2000, domain=domain)
plot_planck2(result, y_frac_window=0.003)

Changing only the temperature to 2.725 K moves the frequency-radiance peak from the infrared/visible-scale solar spectrum into the microwave region.

What it shows

  • The peak frequency is 159.535 GHz, corresponding to c/ν = 1.879 mm.
  • The dimensionless peak remains at x = 2.809720; for a fixed quantity, peak frequency is proportional to temperature.
  • The bolometric radiance drops to 9.952395e-07 W m−2 sr−1, illustrating the strong T4 dependence.

Temperature: 2.725 K  |  Peak frequency: 159.535 GHz  |  Equivalent c/ν: 1.879 mm  |  Relative integral error: -6.899e-07

Notes for evaluation

  • Three quantities: the program supports wavelength radiance Bλ, frequency radiance Bν, and frequency energy density uν, all in SI units.
  • Peak interpretation: wavelength and frequency spectral densities peak at different dimensionless x values. Converting νpeak to c/ν does not recover λpeak.
  • Integration: Planck2 reports both the dimensionless shape area and the physical-coordinate integral with the appropriate Jacobian, along with an exact zero-to-infinity bolometric reference.
  • Display window: y_frac_window=0.003 limits only the plotted horizontal range to the significant part of the curve. It does not change the domain used for peak search or numerical integration.
  • Finite-domain accuracy: these runs use 2,000 intervals over 0.01 ≤ x ≤ 100. Their remaining integral difference includes truncation, trapezoidal discretization, and the selected small- and large-x approximations.
  • Verification: all 94 supplied tests passed from the canonical tests directory before these figures were generated.
