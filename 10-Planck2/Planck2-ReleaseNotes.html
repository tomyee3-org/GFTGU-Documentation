Release Status

Version 1.0.4 concludes a kickoff maintenance review followed by four rounds of independent review involving Codex, Copilot, and Gemini. The reviewers compared the Python implementation with Schutz's original Java source; verified the Planck-law formulas, coordinate transformations, numerical integration, input validation, floating-point behavior, plotting, Help-file accuracy, Version/Build synchronization, and regression coverage; and tested both the canonical and flattened upload layouts. No release-blocking defect remains. Copilot and Gemini both recommend immediate release, and the program is sufficiently robust, accurate, and clear for student use within the tutorial scope and limitations described below.

Major Improvements in This Release

  • Verified the defining physics for wavelength spectral radiance Bλ, frequency spectral radiance Bν, and frequency spectral energy density uν, including their SI prefactors, coordinate Jacobians, units, peak locations, and Stefan–Boltzmann reference integrals.
  • Preserved Schutz's sampled-maximum and trapezoidal-integration investigations while improving the Python program to report the peak, dimensionless shape area, numerical physical integral, and exact infinite-domain bolometric reference together.
  • Corrected the grid semantics so n_steps divides the selected domain into exactly that many intervals, both endpoints are sampled, exactly n_steps trapezoids are accumulated, and the final sample equals x_max.
  • Added stable Rayleigh–Jeans, expm1, and Wien evaluation branches and froze their exact x_low/x_high boundary behavior with adjacent-floating-point tests.
  • Added normalized frexp/ldexp product evaluation so representable final results are not rejected because an intermediate temperature power or product overflows or underflows prematurely.
  • Added systematic validation for invalid quantities, temperatures, exponents, domains, resolutions, step sizes, plot controls, derived physical values, and genuinely unrepresentable floating-point results.
  • Made simulation results immutable and required the reported x_peak, physical peak coordinate, and peak value to identify the same first maximum sample before plotting.
  • Hardened plotting against malformed arrays, scalars, labels, and units; sorted physical coordinates for left-to-right display; and repaired the legal one-point peak-window case.
  • Clarified that the numerical physical integral covers the selected finite domain while the printed and plotted exact bolometric reference covers zero to infinity.
  • Expanded the Help exercises into an approximately progressive sequence covering solar and CMB spectra, Wien scaling, wavelength-versus-frequency peaks, radiance versus energy density, Stefan–Boltzmann scaling, convergence, and asymptotic limits.
  • Added a reproducible BUILD_ID across the four executable source modules, with newline-independent hashing, damaged-source fail-fast behavior, and synchronization across source, Help, runtime output, and result metadata.

Test Suite Growth

A new 59-test regression suite was created at kickoff and expanded to 94 automated tests during the audit process. The suite defines the four core module filenames and uses find_module_dir(start) to locate the nearest directory containing all four, so the same test file works from the canonical tests/ directory or when flattened beside the program modules.

The final suite verifies physical constants and closed forms; wavelength/frequency conversions and Jacobians; all three spectral quantities; analytic and sampled peak positions; solar and CMB reference values; temperature scaling; transformed-spectrum identities; finite-domain trapezoidal integration; small-, intermediate-, and large-x branches; unusual domains, endpoint peaks, one-step grids, and tied maxima; invalid and extreme finite inputs; binary64 overflow, subnormal, and underflow boundaries; immutable and internally consistent results; plotting and annotation behavior; command-line execution; Help structure and scientific content; Python 3.10 compatibility; and independent Build-ID reproduction. All 94 tests pass under CPython 3.10.20 and CPython 3.12.13 in both canonical and flattened upload layouts.

Known Limitations

  • Intended for education and conceptual exploration rather than precision radiometry, instrument calibration, or research-grade spectral modeling.
  • The reported peak is the largest sampled point, not an interpolated or root-solved maximum. Its accuracy is limited by the grid spacing, and it may be a domain endpoint when the mathematical peak lies outside the selected interval.
  • The numerical integral covers the finite x_min-to-x_max domain, whereas the exact bolometric reference covers zero to infinity. Their difference includes finite-domain truncation, trapezoidal discretization, and the selected asymptotic approximations.
  • The default settings give physical-integral relative error below 10−6 in all three modes, but this bound does not apply after the domain, resolution, or approximation thresholds are edited.
  • x_low and x_high deliberately select the Rayleigh–Jeans and Wien approximation regions. Moving them is an educational experiment and can intentionally reduce numerical accuracy.
  • The program accepts any positive finite temperature whose derived results remain representable; it does not impose an arbitrary astrophysical temperature range. Inputs whose final physical values overflow or underflow to unusable zero are rejected.
  • The maximum resolution of 1,000,000 intervals retains three tuples of 1,000,001 Python floating-point values and is therefore intentionally memory- and computation-intensive. Ordinary exercises use much smaller resolutions.
  • The Help file loads MathJax from a public CDN. Offline, its prose and unrendered TeX source remain available, but equations are not typeset.

Known Minor Maintenance Items

  • The regression that protects removal of redundant full-array scans checks the driver's validator call pattern and may need broadening after a harmless future refactor that preserves linear performance.
  • The plotter deliberately checks labels and integral units against the physics-layer metadata helpers. A future metadata change must remain synchronized across result construction, plotting, Help, and tests; a shared immutable quantity descriptor may become useful if additional modes are added.
  • The isolated module suite validates the expected relative links to the Star and Random2 Help files but cannot prove that those targets exist. Repository-level link checking remains appropriate for the complete documentation tree.
  • A future documentation-wide overhaul may bundle MathJax locally if fully typeset offline Help becomes a distribution requirement.
