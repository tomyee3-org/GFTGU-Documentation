Orbit

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.3.1  |  Build 93380f960f8e  |  Generated 5 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide presents four plots generated from the supplied current source. The examples show a Mercury-like bound orbit in three complementary display modes and an unbound trajectory above the local escape speed. No external image files are required.

Orbit follows a test particle in the Newtonian inverse-square field of a fixed central mass. Its adaptive predictor-corrector integrator records position, velocity, specific energy, and specific angular momentum while accumulating azimuthal travel to stop accurately at a requested revolution count.

Figure 1 — Default Mercury-like orbit

result = run_orbit(
    xInit=4.6e10, yInit=0.0,
    vxInit=0.0, vyInit=58_980.0,
    k=GM_SUN, dt0=1.0e4, maxSteps=20_000,
    eps1=0.05, eps2=1.0e-4, maxOrbits=1.0)
plot_orbit(result, output="orbit")

The supplied defaults place the particle at approximate Mercury perihelion with a tangential speed of 58.980 km/s. The solver refines its final step so the accumulated azimuth is exactly one revolution.

What it shows

  • The orbit is elliptical, with the fixed Sun at one focus rather than at the ellipse’s geometric centre.
  • The computed perihelion and aphelion radii are 46.000 and 69.836 million km, corresponding to an estimated eccentricity of 0.2058.

Computed period: 87.991 days  |  Accepted steps: 761  |  Revolutions: 1.00000000  |  Endpoint-refinement trials: 7

Figure 2 — Velocity-space hodograph

plot_orbit(result, output="velocity")

The same integration is displayed in velocity space. For a non-radial Kepler ellipse, the velocity vector traces a circle—the Hamilton hodograph—even though the position-space trajectory is an ellipse.

What it shows

  • The speed is greatest at perihelion and least at aphelion, ranging from 38.850 to 58.980 km/s.
  • The closed velocity-space curve complements the numerical closure diagnostics: both the position and velocity should return close to their starting values after one revolution.

Minimum speed: 38.850 km/s  |  Maximum speed: 58.980 km/s  |  Velocity closure residual: 4.216e-05

Figure 3 — Specific-energy history

plot_orbit(result, output="energy")

Kinetic and potential specific energy exchange throughout the eccentric orbit. Their dashed sum remains nearly constant and negative, as required for a bound Kepler orbit.

What it shows

  • Kinetic energy rises while potential energy becomes more negative near perihelion; the reverse occurs near aphelion.
  • The initial total specific energy is -1.145733 GJ/kg. The program reports the largest fractional departure from that initial value over the whole run.

Maximum fractional energy drift: 5.344e-05  |  Maximum fractional angular-momentum drift: 2.434e-05  |  Radius closure residual: 5.228e-09

Figure 4 — Hyperbolic escape experiment

result = run_orbit(
    xInit=4.6e10, yInit=0.0,
    vxInit=0.0, vyInit=85_000.0,
    k=GM_SUN, dt0=1.0e4, maxSteps=600,
    eps1=0.05, eps2=1.0e-4, maxOrbits=1.0)
plot_orbit(result, output="orbit")

At the same starting radius, the local escape speed is 75.961 km/s. Raising the tangential launch speed to 85.000 km/s gives positive specific energy, so the path is open rather than elliptical.

What it shows

  • The particle never accumulates the requested full revolution. The run therefore ends at the 600 accepted-step limit after 69.44 days.
  • The radius grows from 46.0 to 322.3 million km while the speed falls to 47.735 km/s.

Launch / escape speed: 85.000 / 75.961 km/s  |  Initial specific energy: 727.447 MJ/kg  |  Termination: maximum accepted-step count

Notes for evaluation

  • Five views: the program supports orbit, velocity, position_time, velocity_time, and energy. The two component-versus-time views are omitted here to keep the sampling compact.
  • Model scope: the central body is fixed and point-like. The parameter k is its gravitational parameter GM in m³/s², not its mass in kilograms.
  • Adaptive integration: dt0 is a maximum timestep. The solver can shrink the working step, iterate a trapezoidal corrector, recover toward dt0, and refine a final step to meet the requested accumulated angle.
  • Diagnostics: the returned result includes explicit termination status, conservation drift, accepted-step counts, angular-step rejections, endpoint-refinement trials, and closure residuals.
  • Verification: all 60 supplied tests passed from the canonical tests directory before these figures were generated.
