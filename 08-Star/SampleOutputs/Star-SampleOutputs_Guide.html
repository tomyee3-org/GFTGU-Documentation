Star

Representative Sample Outputs

Version 1.0.3  |  Build 019ff5e3fe2c  |  Generated 5 September 2026

Purpose. This compact, self-contained guide presents the four profiles generated by the supplied current program for its default solar-scale polytropic model: pressure, density, temperature, and enclosed mass versus radius. No external image files are required.

Star integrates Newtonian hydrostatic equilibrium and mass continuity outward through a spherically symmetric polytrope. The default parameters are tuned to give approximately the Sun’s total mass and radius, but the single equation of state is a teaching model rather than a realistic calculation of the Sun’s layered composition, energy generation, or energy transport.

Figure 1 — Pressure profile

result = integrate_star(
    p_c=7.158e15, T_c=2.263e7,
    mu=1.285, gamma=1.36,
    max_points=2000, steps_per_scale=400,
    output_type="pressure")
plot_star_structure(result, log_y=False)

Pressure falls monotonically from the specified central value to the interpolated zero-pressure surface. The returned data include that explicit surface point.

What it shows

  • At approximately one quarter of the modeled radius, pressure is 0.279 of the central value; at one half-radius it is only 0.0156.
  • The steep decline reflects the coupled calculation: enclosed mass strengthens the pressure gradient while the falling pressure lowers the density through the imposed polytropic law.

Central pressure: 7.158e+15 Pa  |  Surface radius: 6.967373×10⁸ m  |  Stored points: 1,325  |  Restart count: 0

Figure 2 — Density profile on a logarithmic axis

result = integrate_star(..., output_type="density")
plot_star_structure(result, log_y=True)

The logarithmic option reveals the low-density outer envelope that is compressed near zero on a linear scale. The exact zero-density surface point cannot appear on a logarithmic axis and is therefore omitted from the plotted curve.

What it shows

  • The ideal-gas central relation gives a density of 49,186.696 kg/m³ for the supplied p_c, T_c, and effective mu.
  • At half-radius the density is 2,308.502 kg/m³; at 90% of the modeled radius it has fallen to 6.671 kg/m³.
  • Throughout the model, density is constrained by one polytropic relation, rho = D p1/gamma.

Central density: 49,186.696 kg/m³  |  Density at 0.5R: 2,308.502 kg/m³  |  Density at 0.9R: 6.671 kg/m³

Figure 3 — Temperature profile

result = integrate_star(..., output_type="temperature")
plot_star_structure(result, log_y=False)

Temperature is recovered from pressure and density through the ideal-gas relation; the program does not solve a separate energy-generation or energy-transport equation.

What it shows

  • The temperature begins at 22.630 million K and falls to 7.523 million K near half-radius.
  • Near 90% of the modeled radius it is 0.917 million K before reaching the formal zero value at the interpolated surface.
  • These temperatures are internally consistent with the toy polytrope, but the default central values should not be interpreted as a standard solar-model profile.

Central temperature: 22.630 MK  |  T(0.5R): 7.523 MK  |  T(0.9R): 0.917 MK

Figure 4 — Enclosed-mass profile

result = integrate_star(..., output_type="mass")
plot_star_structure(result, log_y=False)

Enclosed mass grows outward from zero and approaches the model’s total mass as the density becomes very small in the outer layers.

What it shows

  • By half-radius the model contains 84.42% of its total mass.
  • By three quarters of the radius it contains 99.000%, so the outer quarter contributes relatively little mass despite occupying most of the volume between 0.75R and R.
  • The final fractional shell uses the left-endpoint density consistently with the forward-Euler mass steps, so its small contribution is slightly overestimated.

Total modeled mass: 1.982451e+30 kg  |  Mass inside 0.5R: 84.42%  |  Mass inside 0.75R: 99.000%

Notes for evaluation

  • Polytropic convention: gamma=1.36 is the polytropic exponent, not the conventional index. It corresponds to n = 1/(gamma − 1) = 2.778.
  • Finite-radius condition: the program requires gamma > 1.2; under the model’s assumptions, smaller exponents do not yield an ordinary finite zero-pressure surface.
  • Numerical method: the structure equations use forward Euler steps. The default radial step is 526,453.702 m. If max_points is insufficient, the driver doubles that step and restarts to obtain radial coverage; the default run requires no restart.
  • Surface treatment: when a trial step first crosses zero pressure, the driver linearly interpolates that final pressure segment and appends a surface point with pressure, density, and temperature equal to zero.
  • Verification: all 70 supplied tests passed from the canonical tests directory before these figures were generated.
